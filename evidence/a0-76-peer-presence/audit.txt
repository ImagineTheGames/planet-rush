a0-76 — "the players who stayed are told nothing", and now they are told
================================================================================
OWNER: UI Engineer.  Branch: agent/ui/a0-76-peer-presence.
Captured 2026-08-17 against the local fleet fixture (tests/net/local-fleet.ts):
two ticket-enforcing Machines with the socket-hop pin armed, a Fly-shaped edge in
front of them, a real allocator process — and the shipped client path for BOTH
clients (allocateRoom / joinRoom -> allocatorTransport -> createOnlineSession ->
WebSocketTransport).

The report
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  "do we have any indication when a player loses connection (like for the other
   players that remained in match...) and when they join back as well... we need
   something to indicate that so other players know"

The answer was no. `src/ui/connection-status.ts` and `src/net/link-loss.ts` are
entirely about YOUR OWN socket, so from the other side of a drop a disconnected
player was a ship that simply stopped making good decisions. Rage-quit, lag, a
reconnect in flight and a bot substitution all looked identical from the cockpit,
and each of them calls for a different response — press the advantage, wait, or
stop counting on an ally.


HOW IT WAS CAPTURED (and why from the OTHER seat)
================================================================================
a0-72's probe stands next to the player who dropped and asks whether the server
takes them back. This one stands next to the player who STAYED and asks what
their screen says about it.

  evidence/a0-76-peer-presence/presence-probe.ts

  WATCHER   allocates the room, starts the match, FLIES (a real client sends
            input every rendered frame), and stays. Its session is fed to
            `applyPresenceMessage` — the SAME function `src/main.ts` hands every
            observed message to — so every "banner" line below is what the HUD
            draws, not a re-implementation of it.
  PHONE     joins the same room through the allocator's join route, then
            backgrounds: a socket that stops calling the page back and then dies,
            which is what a suspended tab leaves behind (`transport.state` still
            reads `open`, no `onclose`, no frame). Real wall-clock seconds pass —
            nothing here moves a clock.

  npx vite-node evidence/a0-76-peer-presence/presence-probe.ts -- --away=25000 --label=drop-and-return
  npx vite-node evidence/a0-76-peer-presence/presence-probe.ts -- --abandon=1   --label=abandon
  npx vite-node evidence/a0-76-peer-presence/presence-probe.ts -- --away=25000 --end-match=1 --label=match-over
  npx vite-node evidence/a0-76-peer-presence/presence-probe.ts -- --away=45000 --label=long-away

Every frame below carries THREE things side by side: the banner the watcher's HUD
had, the standing per-seat state behind it, and AUTHORITY'S OWN ROSTER at the same
instant (`room.lobbyState()` + `room.seatHeldForMatch()`), read straight off the
Machine. That third column is the check that the HUD is not making anything up.

Room PBQW throughout; WATCHER is seat 0 (P1), PHONE is seat 1 (P2).


1. DROPPED — "P2 — CONNECTION LOST · BOT FLYING"
================================================================================
  drop-and-return.json

  wire (what authority actually said, ms from boot):
        1     lobbyState
      449     playerSubstituted  { player: 1, graceSeconds: 0, heldForMatch: true }
      449     lobbyState
    25579     playerReclaimed    { player: 1 }
    25579     lobbyState

  t = 0.00 s   match live — two humans flying, nothing to say
    banner    (nothing)
    state     (nothing)
    authority seat 0  bot:no  held:no      seat 1  bot:no  held:no

  t = 0.30 s   their screen went black
    banner    P2 — CONNECTION LOST · BOT FLYING
    row       name "P2" (seat 1's identity colour) · reason "CONNECTION LOST"
              (muted) · clause "BOT FLYING" · alpha 1.00
    state     seat 1  dropped  bot:yes
    authority seat 0  bot:no  held:no      seat 1  bot:YES  held:YES

  The banner and authority agree, character for character, at the same instant:
  the room seated a bot on seat 1 and is holding that seat for its operator for as
  long as the match runs (a0-72's `heldForMatch`). NOTHING in that line was
  inferred from the simulation — the whole of it comes from `playerSubstituted`
  and the `lobbyState` that rides with it.

  t = 6.27 s   six seconds later
    banner    (nothing)
    state     seat 1  dropped  bot:yes
    authority seat 1  bot:YES  held:YES

  THE LINE IS TRANSIENT; THE STATE IS NOT. This is the distinction the whole model
  turns on: five seconds of banner is what a player can read mid-fight, and the
  standing "seat 1 is a bot right now" is what stays true for the twenty-five
  seconds a player spends deciding whether to press the advantage.


2. BACK — "P2 — BACK · BOT OUT"
================================================================================
  drop-and-return.json (same run)

  t = 25.30 s  still away, still held — the moment before they return
    banner    (nothing)
    state     seat 1  dropped  bot:yes
    authority seat 1  bot:YES  held:YES

  t = 25.53 s  they are back
    banner    P2 — BACK · BOT OUT
    row       name "P2" · reason "BACK" · clause "BOT OUT" · alpha 1.00
    state     seat 1  back  bot:no
    authority seat 1  bot:no   held:no

  NAMED AGAIN, AND BY THE SAME NAME. A player who read `P2 — CONNECTION LOST` half
  a minute ago does not have to work out that "seat 1" is the same person: the
  banner spells a seat exactly the way its nameplate and its hull decal do
  (`resolveName`, `P{n}` for a seat with no lobby name), and it is drawn in that
  seat's own identity colour.

  `BOT OUT` is stated rather than left to inference — the substitution was
  announced, so the hand-back is too.

  t = 31.55 s  six seconds later — the seat is an ordinary seat again
    banner    (nothing)
    state     (nothing)
    authority seat 1  bot:no   held:no

  `back` is a thing that happened, not a condition: once its line has been on
  screen for its whole window the seat settles back to `here` and the log holds
  nothing about it at all.


3. GONE FOR GOOD, by their own hand — "P2 — LEFT THE MATCH · BOT FLYING"
================================================================================
  abandon.json

  wire:
        1     lobbyState
      273     playerSubstituted  { player: 1, graceSeconds: 0, heldForMatch: FALSE }
      273     lobbyState

  t = 0.13 s   they pressed ABANDON
    banner    P2 — LEFT THE MATCH · BOT FLYING
    state     seat 1  gone  bot:yes
    authority seat 1  bot:YES  held:NO

  THE SAME MESSAGE CARRIES BOTH, AND `heldForMatch` IS THE WHOLE DIFFERENCE. A
  drop and an abandon arrive as one message type with one seat number on it; a
  peer does different things about them, so the banner says which. `held: NO` on
  the authority row is the fact the copy is reading — there is no window, the
  token is dead, and nobody is coming back to that seat.


4. GONE FOR GOOD, because the match ended — silently
================================================================================
  match-over.json

  wire:
      449     playerSubstituted  { player: 1, graceSeconds: 0, heldForMatch: true }
    25583     matchEnd
    25583     lobbyState

  t =  0.28 s  their screen went black
    banner    P2 — CONNECTION LOST · BOT FLYING
    state     seat 1  dropped  bot:yes
    authority seat 1  bot:YES  held:YES

  t = 25.35 s  the match ended while they were away
    banner    (nothing)
    state     seat 1  GONE  bot:yes
    authority seat 1  bot:YES  held:NO      <-- the hold ended with the match

  The hold a0-72 grants runs for the length of the match and no longer, so a
  player still on their way back has nothing left to come back to. The state
  moves; the banner does not fire. That is deliberate: the end-of-match summary is
  up, and a line announcing a departure over a screen that has already announced
  the result is noise about a match that is over.


5. RETURNING — MODELLED, AND HONESTLY NOT REACHED
================================================================================
This is the one state in the brief's list that no frame above shows, and the
reason is a fact about the wire rather than a gap in the HUD.

Between "the phone starts dialling" and "the phone is back", authority says
NOTHING to anyone else. `server/room.ts` `reclaim()` learns a returning player
exists at the instant their reclaim succeeds, and broadcasts `playerReclaimed` at
that same instant — so there is no in-flight window on the wire for a peer to
observe. Measured across the four runs above, the whole of that window is:

  drop-and-return   playerReclaimed at 25579 ms; the dial began ~360 ms earlier
  long-away         playerReclaimed at 45577 ms; same shape

`PeerPresence` carries `returning` as a first-class state with its own copy
(`RECONNECTING`, the word `src/ui/connection-status.ts` already uses for your own
socket) and its own entry point (`PeerPresenceLog.noteReturning`), so it lights up
the moment authority announces one. **No client-side guess is offered in its
place**: a client cannot know another client is dialling, and inventing that would
be exactly the thing this whole deliverable exists to stop inventing. The PR
carries the one additive broadcast that would light it up; `src/net/` and
`server/` are the Netcode Engineer's files.


6. WHERE IT LANDS — phone to ultrawide
================================================================================
Placement is measured off the wave clock's own DRAWN footprint, at the clock's
real line widths, in the a0-74 content box. Captured in every JSON above.

  viewport                        clock ends   band top   centre    rows, wheel:
                                                                    closed / open
  844x390   phone (landscape)         70 px      75 px     422 px      2  /  0
  390x844   phone (portrait)          70 px      75 px     195 px      2  /  2
  1280x720  desktop 16:9              82 px      88 px     640 px      2  /  2
  3840x1080 ultrawide 32:9            82 px      88 px    1920 px      2  /  2

  * Every band sits INSIDE the HUD margin at every profile (`insideMargin: true`),
    which is what makes the layer's `full` + PAD registration honest rather than
    hopeful.
  * On the ULTRAWIDE the band is centred on 1920, not on 3840/2-of-the-raw-viewport
    — it is laid out in the centred content box (x=960, width=1920) like the clock
    above it, so it stays with the rest of the chrome instead of a head-turn away
    (a0-74).
  * On a 390 px-TALL LANDSCAPE PHONE with the Build wheel open the band shows
    ZERO rows. The wheel is a control the player is pressing right now and the
    banner is transient, so the wheel wins the pixels — the same call a0-24 made
    for the clock itself. Everywhere else the band sheds its OLDEST row rather
    than going silent.
  * The band never reaches the minimap: it is a top-centre element and the map is
    bottom-right (asserted in `src/ui/hud.test.ts`).


7. ONE ARTIFACT OF THE FIXTURE, NAMED
================================================================================
  long-away.json — the 45-second run

    35215     playerReclaimed   { player: 0 }     <-- the WATCHER's own seat

The watcher reconnects at 35.2 s in every run longer than that, and it is the
fixture, not the product: `server/ws.ts` pings an idle socket every
`PING_INTERVAL_MS` (20 s) and destroys it `PONG_TIMEOUT_MS` (15 s) later if no
pong comes back — 35 s exactly — and `tests/net/node-websocket.ts` is a
hand-rolled RFC 6455 client that does not answer protocol pings. A real browser
WebSocket answers them in the browser, below JavaScript. a0-72's probe never saw
this because its single client was the one being suspended. Runs 1–4 above are
therefore captured at a 25-second background, which is under that ceiling and
still a real one: the socket is dead, a bot is flying, and the seat is held.

Kept rather than deleted, because it is also a positive result:

  t = 45.30 s  state  seat 0 back (the watcher's OWN seat)  ·  seat 1 dropped
               banner (nothing about seat 0)

Your own drop is `src/net/link-loss`'s full-screen card with RECONNECT and ABANDON
on it. The log tracks the local seat's state and DRAWS NOTHING for it, so a
second, quieter copy of that card never appears under the wave clock. The 45 s run
is the only place in this evidence where that suppression is exercised against a
real reconnect.


WHAT THE PLAYER GETS
================================================================================
  a seat drops        P2 — CONNECTION LOST · BOT FLYING
  a seat comes back   P2 — BACK · BOT OUT
  a seat abandons     P2 — LEFT THE MATCH · BOT FLYING
  a seat is reclaimed by the match ending          (state only, no line)
  your own seat       (nothing — that is the overlay's job)

Five seconds a line, top centre under the wave clock, newest first, at most three
at once, in the seat's own identity colour. Plain and diagnostic, per GDD §4.7's
match/machine line — a dropped socket is a machine fact, and the mining authority
has no voice for it.
