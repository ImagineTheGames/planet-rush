===============================================================
Branch  agent/ui/a0-77-settings-help

        private port (PREVIEW_PORT=4277), because several lanes share this box

THE REPORT
----------
Developer, 2026-08-17, with a screenshot of SETTINGS on a phone:

  "id like to see ? marks that are clickable on PC and mobile next to each
   button somewhere convenient that shows a pop up and explains what that
   toggle/button does..."

Before this branch a player read `FIRE MODE · AUTO-AIM` and `CONTROLS · TAP
COMMANDER` and had to change them mid-match to find out what they did. Two of
those rows change how the game PLAYS.


1. WHAT IS ON THE SCREEN NOW
----------------------------
Every row in `SETTINGS_ROWS` carries a `?` on its LEADING edge that opens a
compact panel naming the row and saying what it does. Six rows, six `?`, no
exceptions — and the count is enforced twice over (§5).

The panel is the LOBBY'S codex tooltip (`src/ui/codex-hint-view.ts`), reused
whole, as the brief instructed: it was already "a compact panel (title, badges,
one-line summary) that floats near the row it describes, clamped inside the
viewport, and hides on any tap". There is one popup in this game, not two.


2. WHY THE `?` LEADS THE ROW  (the measurement the brief asks for)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief: *"must not overlap the value or the steppers at the narrowest
supported width… The screenshot is a phone in landscape at 798×384 — check that
width specifically."*

MEASURED at 798×384, read off the client (`readback.json`, phone-landscape):

  the row                       x=23    w=372   h=55     (two columns)
  the row's `?`                 x=23    w=48    h=48
  the − stepper (MASTER VOLUME) x=673   w=48
  the + stepper (MASTER VOLUME) x=727   w=48
  clear space between them      222 px  ← the label and the ten pips live here

  The `?` ends at x=71. The nearest thing it could crowd is 222 px away. On the
  toggle rows the value chip is right-aligned and 149–160 px wide, so it starts
  at x≈229 — 158 px clear.

At the other two profiles, the same three numbers:

  profile                        row w   `?` w × h   `?` end → − stepper
  -----------------------------  -----   ---------   ------------------
  phone landscape 798×384          372     48 × 48        222 px
  phone PORTRAIT held (844×390)    394     48 × 48        244 px
  desktop 1280×800                 680     48 × 48        530 px

WHY IT IS THAT EDGE, and it is not a preference: the TRAILING edge is spoken for
on every row — a toggle's value chip is there, and a volume's two 48px steppers
are. At 372 px there is nothing to take from either without dropping a control
under the thumb floor (`TOUCH_MIN` = 48). The leading edge is the one place on a
settings row where nothing that takes a press already lives, so "clears the value
and the steppers" is true BY CONSTRUCTION at every width rather than by a
measurement a future label could invalidate.

WHAT IT IS NOT: a 12-px glyph. The `?` is the same 48×48 square the −/+ steppers
are (`valueChipHeight`, floored at `TOUCH_MIN`), at every profile above — the
brief's "a real touch-target size", asserted per row per width in
`src/ui/settings.test.ts`.

WHAT PAID FOR IT, stated plainly: on a volume row the pip readout gives up the
handoff's fixed 176-px label column once that column would leave the pips under
90 px (`PIPS_MIN_WIDTH`). The label's MEASURED width is still a hard floor, so
nothing is ever written over; the desktop still honours the fixed column exactly.
No CONTROL shrank — only the readout, and only where the width demanded it. The
frames are the check: ten pips are still ten legible pips at 798×384
(`shots/phone-landscape-798x384-rest.png`).


3. BOTH INPUT SCHEMES, DRIVEN THROUGH THE REAL CLIENT
-----------------------------------------------------
`__mainMenu.settingsHelpTitle` reports the TITLE of the open explanation, from
the same model the view draws — so this table is what the screen actually said
after each real gesture, not what the harness hoped. Identical at all three
profiles (`readback.json` → `grammar`):

  gesture                                        the screen then said
  --------------------------------------------   --------------------
  (at rest)                                      —
  press/tap the `?` on MASTER VOLUME             MASTER VOLUME
  press/tap the SAME `?` again                   —            (toggles closed)
  press/tap away from every control              —            (tap away closes)
  ArrowDown, then Enter — no pointer at all      SFX VOLUME
  Escape                                         —
  …and the screen is still                       settings     (Escape took the
                                                              panel, not the screen)

The touch profiles ran that whole table with `page.touchscreen.tap` and the
desktop with `page.mouse.click`: "on pointer and on touch" is two claims, and a
capture of one is not evidence of the other.

WHY THE KEYBOARD ROW SAYS *SFX* rather than FIRE MODE: the focus ring follows a
POINTER press, so a player who clicks a `?` and then reaches for the arrows
carries on from the row their hand was on — here, MASTER VOLUME + 1. A finger
never moves the ring (a touch device has no keyboard to move it with), which is
why the touch frames show no ring and `phone-landscape-798x384-keyboard.png`,
driven by arrows alone, does.

KEYBOARD, in full: ArrowDown / ArrowUp (or W/S, or Tab) walk the column of `?`
controls and wrap at both ends; Enter or Space opens the focused one; Escape
closes the panel first and the screen second. The focus is drawn as a RING inside
the chip rather than as the hover state, so "the pointer is here" and "the
keyboard is here" stay two different pictures on a screen a player may be driving
with both.

ALL TWELVE WERE OPENED AND READ AT FULL SIZE BEFORE COMMITTING.

WHAT THEY SHOW THAT IS WORTH SAYING OUT LOUD: a floating panel on a 384-px-tall
phone overlaps its NEIGHBOURS. It does not overlap the row it explains — when
there is no room above the `?`, the panel is re-anchored below the whole ROW
rather than below the `?`, so the value the player just asked about is never the
thing the answer covers. It is transient and any tap dismisses it, which is the
same grammar the lobby's dossier has kept since a0-06.


5. THE GATE — why a seventh row cannot ship without copy
--------------------------------------------------------
Two halves, and neither is "today's six rows have text":

  tsc      `SETTINGS_HELP` is a `Record<SettingsRowKey, SettingsHelp>` and
           `SettingsRowKey` is DERIVED from `SettingsRowSpec`. A new row kind
           does not compile until it has an entry.
  vitest   `every row explains itself` (src/ui/settings.test.ts) walks
           `SETTINGS_ROWS` itself and asserts, per row: there is copy; the
           summary is a sentence and not a placeholder; the panel's title is the
           row's OWN label (a panel a player cannot tie to the control they
           tapped is not an explanation); no two rows share a summary; the
           summary stays inside 260 characters (GDD §4.7 — "length is part of
           clarity"); and no key in the register is an orphan.

So a row added later without help text fails the BUILD, and a row given empty or
mis-titled copy fails the TEST. That is the point of it: not that today's six
have text, but that a seventh cannot arrive without it.


6. THE COPY, AND WHERE EACH FACT COMES FROM
-------------------------------------------
Written to GDD §4.7 register 2: procedural, present tense, second person, no
adjective that sells a setting. Each says what the control does and when to want
it; none says how it is implemented.

  FIRE MODE       AUTO-AIM takes the aim, never the trigger — GDD §2.4 ("the
                  player decides *when* to fire, positioning decides *what* gets
                  hit"), and it leads a moving target because the shot has
                  travel time. MANUAL names all three aim devices, because the
                  mode is a player setting on every platform.
  CONTROLS        The two schemes as HANDS. Deliberately the same lesson a0-33's
                  in-match tip teaches: its Tap Commander wordings are "Tap the
                  asteroid to mine it" and "tap your own station to bank in its
                  collection field", and this row says the same gesture one
                  level up. It says "the other scheme" rather than naming it,
                  because the row's own word for it is the DEVICE in front of
                  the player (u8-01: STICKS / TWIN STICKS / KEYBOARD + MOUSE) —
                  so it names all three hardware forms instead, which is true on
                  every device.
  REDUCE VFX      What it thins, and that the game ALSO does it unasked below
                  30 fps (`VfxAutoQuality`, GDD §4.8 risk 5). That second half is
                  the thing a player watching effects thin out mid-fight cannot
                  otherwise account for.
  MASTER VOLUME   Everything, alarm included — `../art/audio/graph` sums every
                  bus into master.
  SFX VOLUME      Weapons, impacts, engines, interface — and that the
                  under-attack alarm is NOT on this channel. It is not: the alarm
                  has its own bus precisely so a slider cannot switch off a
                  mechanic on the not-cuttable list (§2.2, §4.9;
                  `audio.test.ts` holds the mixer to it).
  MUSIC VOLUME    The soundtrack only, and that nothing you need in a fight
                  rides it.


7. WHAT WAS **NOT** DONE, and why
---------------------------------
- **The lobby's own toggles and the pause menu's settings did not get their own
  treatment.** The brief forbids widening to them; both are proposed as
  follow-ups in the PR body. The one thing that WAS wired beyond the brief's
  screen is the pause settings screen's TAP, and only because it is literally the
  same `SettingsView`: it now draws six `?`, and a drawn control that does
  nothing is worse than no control. Its keyboard column is deliberately not
  wired — in a match the arrow keys fly the ship.
- **No hue was spent.** The `?` is bone on the same `secondary` chip the
  steppers wear; yellow still means ore and red still means damage
  (style-guide §2), and a help affordance is neither.
- **DONE is still the screen's one bright plate** (`singlePrimary`, asserted in
  `settings.test.ts`) — the `?` is a chip, not a plate.
