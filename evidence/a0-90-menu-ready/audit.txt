a0-90 — "the title menu looks ready well before it is"
======================================================

Developer, 2026-08-19, from live play:

  "there is a pause after the title animation plays and being able to click on
   a button (theres a literal PING that you hear and it takes so long to be
   able to actually click)"

Everything below is measured on the real `vite preview` bundle, not on a dev
server and not in jsdom. Harness: `measure.mjs` in this directory (Playwright,
Chromium, the two device profiles QA's matrix names — desktop 1280x800 dpr 1 no
touch, iPhone-ish 390x844 dpr 3 `isMobile`).

  node measure.mjs                      # timelines + the reseal path
  CLICKS=1 DELAYS=2500,2900,... node measure.mjs
  SHOTS=shots SHOT_AT=2900,3200 TAG=before node measure.mjs
  BASE=http://localhost:4198/ ...       # point at another bundle

`before` is `main` at a763ab7; `after` is this branch. Both were built with
`vite build` and served with `vite preview`, side by side on two ports, so the
two columns differ in one file.


1. THE THREE TIMINGS THE BRIEF ASKED FOR
----------------------------------------

Milliseconds from the opening press (the `pointerdown` the page itself stamped,
not the one the driver asked for).

  "animation visually settles" = the frame the LAST GATE PIXEL leaves the
  screen. Read two independent ways: (a) the punched star-field canvas reports
  alpha 0 at all four corners — the picture itself; (b) the door's live
  computed transform, run through the shipped `openingPolygon`, contains all
  four corners of the viewport. The doorway is convex, so corners are the whole
  rectangle. (a) is the number quoted; (b) agrees within one sample.

  "the sound plays"     = the audible instant of the last cue cluster scheduled
                          after the press (`gateSeated`, the confirm triad),
                          taken by wrapping OscillatorNode/AudioBufferSourceNode
                          `.start(when)` and correcting `when` against
                          `ctx.currentTime`.

  "phase === locked"    = the phase in which the gate stops being modal and
                          stops eating presses. On the way IN that is `open`,
                          read off the overlay's own `pointer-events:none`.
                          (`locked` is the SEALED phase; a boot starts there and
                          never returns to it without `Escape` — see §3.)

                     settles      PING     takes a press     dead window
  BEFORE
    desktop            2 955     3 536           3 600           645 ms
    phone              3 061     3 552           3 553           492 ms
  AFTER
    desktop            2 983     2 917           2 983             0 ms
    phone              3 305     3 069           3 305             0 ms

  Read the AFTER rows as "the same instant, three times". The PING is raised by
  `through()`, in the same synchronous call as the phase change that opens the
  menu, so the two are one event in code; the small negative offsets are the
  sampler's own lag on a software-GL runner whose main thread is saturated
  (~43 ms per frame desktop, ~240 ms phone). "settles" and "takes a press" are
  read by two different samplers and land on the same millisecond because after
  this change they are the same frame by construction.

  The 3.0 s / 3.3 s that remains is the door — four beats the player watches,
  which is the screen. What is gone is the part after it.

  The 2 500 column is refused in both columns and must be: the door is still on
  the screen there.


3. THE BRIEF'S LEAD, MEASURED — AND WHY IT IS NOT THIS
------------------------------------------------------

The brief pointed at `pollSeal` waiting out `SEAL_CAP_MS = 3200` because `home`
never arrives. That is not what is happening, for two reasons:

  a. `pollSeal` is only ever reached from phase `closing`, and the only thing
     that produces `closing` is `reseal()`, and the only thing that calls
     `reseal()` is `Escape`. A boot starts at `locked` (`title-gate.ts`, the
     field initialiser) and goes `locked -> turning -> parting -> entering ->
     open`. The seal is never polled on the path the developer described.

  b. On the path where it IS polled, `home` arrives. Escape to lock, measured:

       desktop   2 610 / 2 559 / 2 593 ms
       phone     3 599 / 3 672 / 3 746 ms

     The reseal steps are `returning` at 0 and `closing` at 1 460, and the
     leaves' own transition is 1 020 ms, so the leaves come home ~2 480 ms after
     the Escape. The cap would put it at ~4 660 ms. Every run above is the
     leaves, not the cap. (The phone's extra ~1 s is `pollSeal` counting FRAMES
     at an assumed 60 Hz on a runner rendering at ~6 fps — the floor, not the
     cap, and not this brief.)

So the lead's mechanism is right and its location is wrong: beat 4 on the way IN
was hung on a timer while the door itself had already left the screen. Which is
the brief's own closing note, applied to the other end of the sequence.


4. WHY THE GAP EXISTED
----------------------

`throughScale()` grows the door until its opening is 1.4x past "exactly off
screen". That margin is deliberate and correct — it is what lets the frame's
fade begin with nothing of the frame left to fade (trap 2). But it means the
door clears the viewport partway through beat 3's 1 500 ms growth, and the rest
of that growth is a door moving where nobody can see it:

  viewport      through-scale   clears at   fraction of the growth
  1920x1080          2.254         1.861            0.687
  1280x800           2.054         1.740            0.702
  844x390 (phone)    2.744         2.124            0.645
  2560x1080          3.005         2.264            0.630
  1024x768           2.229         1.691            0.563

Beat 4 was scheduled at 3 460 ms = 1 880 + 1 500 + 80: the END of that growth.
The last ~30-45% of it, plus the 80 ms tail, is the dead window.


5. WHAT CHANGED
---------------

`pollThrough()` runs each frame of `entering` and asks the picture, using the
geometry the punch is already made of: does `openingPolygon` at the door's
MEASURED scale contain all four corners of the viewport (`gateClearsViewport`)?
The first frame it does, the punch has erased the entire field and the hull ring
is past every edge — so the gate goes through then.

Kept, deliberately:
  - the 1.4 margin (trap 2);
  - the timed 3 460 step, demoted to a CAP — the fallback for a tab that never
    ran the transition, which is exactly what `SEAL_CAP_MS` already is at the
    other end. A `null` measurement is NOT read as arrival: a leaf that cannot
    be measured has nowhere to be but shut, while a door that cannot be measured
    may be anywhere.

Not done, deliberately:
  - shortening the cap, which would hide the bug rather than fix it;
  - accepting presses during the animation and queueing them, which the brief
    rules out and which trades a dead menu for a menu that fires late.


6. THE PICTURE IS THE SAME; ONLY THE ANSWER CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------

`shots/` holds four instants, before and after, on both devices. The eight PNGs
are byte-identical per device:

  41d7136591f27b6fd1da091205d8098f  {before,after}-desktop-{2900,3200}ms.png
  20d127a76da8401aca9167d99cdc6566  {before,after}-phone-{2900,3200}ms.png

Four frames, one picture each: a finished main menu, no door, nothing moving.
In the BEFORE column three of those four refuse a press. That is the bug in one
checksum — the screen was never wrong, it was only lying about being ready.


7. WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
----------------------

  - These are emulated devices in headless Chromium on a software GL runner, not
    a handset. The numbers that matter here are CSS-transition and timer
    instants, which advance on the wall clock and do not depend on frame rate;
    the SAMPLING of them does, and the resolution is stated above (~43 ms
    desktop, ~240 ms phone). A real 60 Hz phone resolves the same instants more
    finely, in the same places.
  - One frame's worth of quantisation survives at the handover, and must: the
    door leaves the screen BETWEEN two frames whatever we do, so the last frame
    that still shows it shows one frame's worth of it. On a device dropping
    frames that last sliver is proportionally larger. It is the same quantum the
    reseal's `pollSeal` has always had.
  - `paintClear` reads null in the AFTER runs. That is not a stale field on the
    screen: `tick` returns before `paint` on the frame it goes through, and the
    overlay is hidden in that same synchronous call, so the canvas is never
    SHOWN unpunched. It simply means the frame loop ended before a fully-punched
    canvas was ever painted — there was no longer any reason to paint one.
