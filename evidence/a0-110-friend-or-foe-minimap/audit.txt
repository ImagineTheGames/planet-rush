4. WHY THE PALETTE WAS A LOOKUP AND NOT A CHOICE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The brief framed the friendly blue as open: plasma-with-overlap, or a distinct
blue that does not collide with it. It is not open. Both halves are already
ratified in this lane, in ONE table, worn by a label that floats over the very
hull whose mark this is:

  src/ui/lobby.ts  SIDE_COLORS = { friendly: PALETTE.plasma  #4DC3FF,
                                   enemy:    #CB7979,
                                   neutral:  PALETTE.patina }

u3, 2026-08-05: "perhaps just Friendly, and Enemy, with colors like Blue for
Friendly, Red for Enemy". And src/art/vfx/shots.ts carries the same ruling for
projectiles in the developer's own words: "all friendlies should have that same
blue and all enemies red... even in team mode... in ffa its all red obviously".

a0-110 is that rule reaching a third surface. A distinct "minimap blue" would
have meant the nameplate over a hull and the mark under it wore two different
blues for one fact — which is the drift that table's doc-comment exists to stop.

THE PLASMA/ENERGY OVERLAP, argued rather than assumed. Accepted. Nothing on this
map draws energy — no shield, no torch, no bolt — so the hue is unambiguous here.
And across surfaces it is not an overlap at all: a friendly bolt is already
plasma, so a friendly mark in plasma is one meaning, not two.

REJECTED, raw threat red #B23A3A for hostiles. It is 3.2:1 on Vacuum and
lobby.test.ts pins every side colour at >= 4.5:1. u3 rejected that rung as too
dim for a 12px word; a hostile mark on the 80px corner map is ~3px across and
needs the legible rung more, not less. #CB7979 is the declared shotEnemy2 step of
the enemy-fire ramp, so NO SEVENTH HUE enters the palette, and red-for-enemy
stays inside the RESERVED rule by the same reasoning the developer ratified for
explosions on 2026-08-18 ("we already use red for enemies and red can also mean
death") — red is reserved to a STATE, and an enemy is that state.

THE PALETTE AUDIT. src/art/compliance.ts walks the SPRITE IR only and does not
reach UI colours, so it neither blesses nor rejects this. The audit that DOES
bind is lobby.test.ts's SIDE_COLORS pin — the hexes plus the >= 4.5:1 contrast
bar — and reusing the table means a0-110 passes it by construction rather than
routing around it. src/ui/lobby.ts's "where this colour may land" list is amended
in this PR to admit the minimap, with the reason it is a real extension: it is
the one surface with no room for a WORD, so there the hue is the sole carrier
rather than reinforcement.


5. THE SIZE, AND WHY IT READ TOO BIG
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SHIP_DOT_FRACTION       0.026 -> 0.0195   (0.75x)
  SATELLITE_DOT_FRACTION  0.020 -> 0.015    (0.75x)
  every floor             UNCHANGED

The cause is not in those numbers. Since a0-88 a ship is a TRIANGLE, and a
triangle's ink is not bounded by its radius: its nose reaches 1.95r and its
overall length is 2.86r. So the ship carrying the SMALLER radius was drawing the
LONGER mark:

  rect      ship r        triangle length     station square side
  80 (touch)  2.080 -> 1.560   5.96 -> 4.47 px      5.67 px (unchanged)
  148 (desk)  3.848 -> 2.886  11.02 -> 8.26 px     10.49 px (unchanged)
  44 (clamped) 1.500 -> 1.500   4.30 -> 4.30 px      3.54 px (unchanged)

At 80px a ship was 5.96px long against a 5.67px station square — it out-reached
the anchor it is supposed to read smaller than. After, it is 4.47 against 5.67.

THE FLOOR DID NOT MOVE, deliberately. The brief's constraint is that a mark
shrunk below legibility has been deleted rather than reduced, so the fraction
took the whole quarter and SHIP_DOT_MIN was left exactly where it was. The 44px
clamped square is byte-identical before and after — it was already floored at
0.026 (0.026 x 44 = 1.14, under the floor), so the quarter lands on every map
anyone plays on and takes nothing from the one map that had nothing to give.

THE SATELLITE HAD TO MOVE TOO, and the brief licenses saying so. Left at 0.020
against a ship at 0.0195 it would have come out LARGER than the ship it is
documented to read smaller than, inverting minimap.ts's own grammar. The same
0.75x keeps the ratio exact. Station (0.04) and ore (0.011) do NOT move — they
are the anchors, and not what was complained about.

Total mark ink in the census fell 4.4% (phone collapsed) to 6.6% (desktop
expanded), which is what a quarter off ships and satellites looks like while
stations and ore hold.


6. THE DEFECT THE CAPTURE CAUGHT — and the unit tests could not
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The first `after` capture came back with the player's OWN ship still dark roster
azure and still full size, while every ally beside it was plasma and a quarter
smaller. 100 green unit tests, and the one mark the player looks at first was the
one mark the ruling never reached.

src/ui/minimap-view.ts redraws the LOCAL ship every frame — it cannot read
scene.ownDot, because the scene rebuilds only every MINIMAP_REDRAW_TICKS and a
dot that tracks your motion has to be current. It recomputed the mark, with the
numbers re-typed as literals:

    const r = Math.max(1.5, size * 0.026) * 1.55;
    color: playerColor(local.owner),

Fixed at the source rather than by re-typing the new numbers a second time:
shipDotRadius() is now the ONE place the ship fraction and its floor are applied,
and the colour goes through the same minimapMarkColor(minimapSide(...)) the scene
uses. A unit test pins the scene against both helpers at three rect sizes, so a
future fork shows up there instead of on a phone.

AND THE RIM. The own mark is stroked so the eye finds "me", and that stroke was
PALETTE.plasma over a roster fill. With the fill now plasma it would have stroked
plasma onto plasma and drawn nothing — an affordance lost silently rather than
visibly. It steps one rung up the same hue to plasmaHot (the declared torch
centre), so it is a highlight on the friendly mark and not a second colour, and
no hue is added. Size (1.55x) still does most of the "that one is me" work.

This is the entire reason the evidence step exists, and it is written down here
because "the tests were green" was true and insufficient.


7. THE ONE DEVIATION FROM a0-88's CAPTURE RULE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
a0-88's rule: a capture script must use only seams that exist on origin/main,
because a script that needs the change cannot photograph the thing before it.

The minimap renders only the player's SENSED state (feature f1). Two seconds into
the frozen scene a viewer senses their own home and nothing else — a0-88's own
readback for this scene records `ship 0`. So a capture of "friendlies blue,
enemies red" on the stock scene is a picture of one blue square.

No existing seam gives the frame either: __nameplateStage.stageBot,
__minimapStage.buildSatellite and __teamFogStage.stage all address the FIRST
non-local ship, so they fight over one slot and no composition yields two
distinct rivals.

So __minimapStage.stageSides() is new, and it is ISOLATED IN ITS OWN COMMIT so a
reviewer can cherry-pick it onto main and confirm what it is: one file, sim
positions and one zeroed core, no colour, no radius, no side logic. The `before`
tree is origin/main + that sha and nothing else. Everything that decides how a
mark is coloured or sized remains the branch's difference alone, and the fog, the
allegiance and the painting are all still done by the shipped pipeline.


8. THE GOLDEN RE-BASELINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A TRAP WORTH RECORDING, because it nearly shipped a stale baseline as a green
one. `npx playwright test --update-snapshots` on this Playwright (1.49.1) DOES
NOT REWRITE A SNAPSHOT THAT PASSES. Run against the changed build it reported
"50 passed" and rewrote zero files, and `git status` was clean — which reads
exactly like "nothing changed" and is not the same claim at all.

What was actually true: every in-match golden holds the corner map, the map's own
station and ship marks did change hue and size, and the delta sat under the
suite's `maxDiffPixelRatio: 0.01` gate. The baselines were stale AND green.

Confirmed by mtime — no snapshot file was touched by the -u run — and settled the
way a0-103 settled it: the affected baselines were DELETED and regenerated, which
is the only path that actually writes.
