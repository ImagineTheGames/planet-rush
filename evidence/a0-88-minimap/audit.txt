4. THE RINGS — WHAT A PLAYER NOW UNDERSTANDS THAT THEY DID NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The station's ring was NOT cut. It was unreadable for a nameable reason: a circle
centred on a body reads as an attribute OF that body, and this one had no
attribute anybody could name — a station does not have a radius the way a ship
has a heading. So the map drew a shape that invited a question it could not
answer. Worse, two washes overlapped and double-blended into a bright lens
exactly where the discs met, which is the map advertising "these are two separate
circles".

Both discs are still drawn. What changed is that they are drawn as ONE THING: the
union of everything sensing for your side (`sensedRegions`), filled once and
outlined once. The union is centred on nothing, so the attribute-of-a-body
reading is not available; it is one even brightness, so there is no lens; and it
changes shape as you fly, which a property of a body would not.

What the player can now read off it, that they could not before:

  · THE LINE MEANS ONE THING — the edge of what your side can see. Not "my ship's
    range" and "my station's range" and a mystery, but one boundary with one
    meaning.
  · WHERE YOU ARE STANDING SHOWS IN ITS SHAPE. Parked at home, the station's 300
    disc is swallowed whole by the ship's 520 and the region is a single circle:
    the second ring is gone because at that moment it genuinely told you nothing —
    which is exactly why the developer could not name it. That is not a deletion,
    it is the answer.
  · FLY OUT AND YOUR HOME'S OWN EYES APPEAR AS A SECOND LOBE. `underway-2x`:
    the ship 955.8 units out (recorded at the instant of the shot), and the region
    is now two pieces — the bubble you are carrying, and the patch your home is
    still watching, with your station's square sitting in the middle of it. The
    coverage count is 2 in both halves; before, that fact was two circles you
    could not tell apart, and now it is a picture of your own sensor cover.
  · IN TEAMS IT IS YOUR SIDE'S SILHOUETTE. `evidence/a0-88-minimap/goldens/
    desktop-frozen-teams.png`: eight overlapping rings become one scalloped
    outline with four squares and four triangles inside it.

If the answer had been "nothing", the brief said to cut it. It is not nothing, so
it stayed — drawn as the one fact it is.

The underway frames are LIVE, not frozen, and that is deliberate: no seam moves
the ship. It flies with a real Tap Commander tap on empty space (the default
scheme, GDD §2.4 amended 2026-08-12) until it is 700+ units out, and the distance
is re-read at the instant of every screenshot — a ship that dies out there
respawns AT home, which would quietly put the frame back at the scene it exists
to contrast with. Both halves landed within 0.2 units of each other (955.96
before, 955.83 after) because the same waypoint chain drives both. The two halves
are separate flights of a live match, so the rival ships in them are not in the
same places; everything the frames are evidence FOR — my ship, my station, the
two coverage lobes, the ore — is in both.

A POCKET IS NOT A PLACE YOU CAN SEE (found while drawing the union, fixed here)
Three or more of a side's discs can RING an area without covering it — four ships
900 apart, each with the shipped 520 sensor, leave the middle of the square 636
from every one of them. The union's boundary then has an inner loop, and the
first cut filled every loop it was handed: the pocket took the wash twice and came
out BRIGHTER than the sensed area around it, the map's most confident "you can
see here" over the one patch nobody is looking at. GDD §2.2 settled what that
costs about a different display: a readout whose unknown state is
indistinguishable from its known one asserts a false state rather than
withholding a true one. `sensedRegions` now returns `{outline, holes}`, sorted by
nesting depth, and the view cuts the pocket back out. Two unit tests, on the
shipped radii. No golden scene contains one — proven, not assumed: see §5.

8. THE CI RED, AND WHY THE UNION HAD TO BE PHASE-LOCKED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PR #463 came back red on shard 2/6. Not a golden, and not one of this brief's
tests: `tests/mobile/team-fog-offline.spec.ts:510` (QA's, desktop only), which
asserts that no pixel the human could see ALONE goes dark when they gain
teammates — "the team read is a UNION". It expected `ffaOnly` to be 0 and got 2.

DIAGNOSED OFF THE ARTIFACT, NOT GUESSED. The shard-2 report was pulled
(`gh api .../actions/artifacts/9348745854/zip`), and the spec's own `comparePair`
re-run in python over its `expanded` rect (360,100,560x560) against the attached
TEAMS/FFA frames. That named the two pixels:

  pixel      FFA (alone)              TEAMS (with allies)
  (864,266)  rgb(19,31,41) lum 29.17  rgb(13,16,21) lum 15.72
  (891,281)  rgb(19,31,41) lum 29.17  rgb(13,16,21) lum 15.72
                    ^ threshold is 26          ^ bare fog

An ASCII dump of a 46x25 neighbourhood in both frames puts the boundary arc in
EXACTLY the same place. Only the anti-aliased outer fringe flickers. So the
union had not shrunk — the geometry was right and the arithmetic was wrong.

THE DEFECT. `tessellate()` spaced its vertices along each free ARC:
`from + sweep*k/steps`. Every chord of a polygonised arc sits a sagitta inside
the true arc, so the drawn edge's exact position depends on chord PHASE. When an
ally's disc clips one side of the human's rim, that free arc's `from` moves, and
every chord all the way round re-phases with it — including along the stretch of
rim the ally never reached. The 1 px edge line slides a fraction of a pixel down
boundary that never moved, and its AA fringe drops back under the "lit?"
threshold wherever it was already marginal. Two pixels were marginal.

THE FIX. `arcStep(radius)` -> `arcSteps(radius)`, a COUNT around the whole
circle, a function of the radius ALONE and never of the arc being drawn.
`tessellate` now emits `from`, then the strictly-interior grid angles
`k*TAU/arcSteps(r)` anchored at angle 0 in the disc's OWN frame, then `to`.
Vertex density is unchanged (16..256 steps, 1.5 px chord target). An untouched
stretch of rim now tessellates to the same points however the rest of the circle
is carved up, so shared boundary renders identically and only genuinely-moved
boundary moves.

REJECTED: a circumscribing polygon (push each vertex out by 1/cos(step/2)).
It would also have made the union a superset of the true discs and so also
turned the red green, but it makes the wash claim ~0.01 px of arena nobody
senses. The wash means *sensed*; over-claiming is the wrong direction (GDD §2.2).
Phase-locking fixes the actual defect without moving the boundary outward at all.
