================================================================================
a0-75 — THE BIGGER THE WINDOW, THE WORSE IT RUNS
Fill-rate audit. Art Agent, 2026-08-17. Branch agent/art/a0-75-fill-rate.
================================================================================

The developer handed over a complete diagnosis of the class of bug before we had
any instrument on it:

    "for the host everything is super choppy"
    "the stutter was only on pc, i tried switching hosts to be the mobile phone
     but only pc has this stuter, i think it gets worse the larger the playing
     area is on my screen"
    "yes it does, if i resize to a small window the game plays much better"

Not the host, not the network, not entity count. It scales with rendered area.
This audit measures that, attributes it, fixes it, and states the budget the fix
holds — including at the 32:9 the developer owns a screen for.

VERDICT IN ONE PARAGRAPH. It is per-pixel cost, and the backdrop is 61% of it at
1280x720 and 95% at 5120x1440. Of the backdrop, the SKY is three quarters: nine
alpha-blended gradient clots, each covering ~40% of the frame, stacked, redrawn
every frame. On top of that the sky's coverage grew with the frame's ASPECT
RATIO as well as its area — 3.03x at 16:9, 4.07x at 21:9, 6.06x at 32:9 — which
is a defect nobody had looked for and which lands hardest on exactly the screen
the report came from. Both are fixed. Backdrop fill is now 2.33 blended
screenfuls per frame at EVERY viewport and on every map, against 5.40 at the
developer's 3440x1440 and 7.39 at 5120x1440.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE INSTRUMENT, AND WHAT IT CANNOT SAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The studio image has no GPU. Chromium here draws WebGL through SwiftShader
(reported: "ANGLE (Google, Vulkan 1.3.0 (SwiftShader Device (Subzero)),
SwiftShader driver)"), a CPU rasteriser.

    NO MILLISECOND IN THIS FILE IS THE DEVELOPER'S MILLISECOND.

What SwiftShader is unusually good for is the axis a0-75 is about: it is a pure
function of work done, with no fixed-function fill headroom to hide behind, and
an A/B measured back-to-back in one page under one load is a real A/B. What it
gets WRONG is the balance between fill and geometry — a CPU rasteriser pays
per-triangle setup that a GPU absorbs, so it over-weights the star field, which
is 3,800-54,000 tiny circles, relative to the sky, which is 9-22 huge ones.

So the load-bearing evidence for WHICH LAYER is not a millisecond. It is
`overdraw.ts`, which walks the shipped `SpriteDef`s and sums the area every shape
asks the rasteriser to shade. That needs no GPU at all, so a fragment counted
here is the same fragment on the developer's ultrawide. Milliseconds are used for
two things only: to show that the shipped bundle's frame time is explained by a
FRAGMENT model and not by a pixel model (section 1), and to A/B the fix under
identical load (section 4).

Files (all under evidence/a0-75-fill-rate/, none in the shipped bundle):

    overdraw.ts        counted fill, no GPU        npx vite-node <path>
    sweep.mjs          shipped bundle, 6 viewports node <path>
    fill-rig.ts/.html  the void layer by layer     driven by attribute.mjs
    attribute.mjs      the sweep + the raw/baked A/B
    cache-diff.ts/.mjs the baked sky vs the sky, pixel for pixel, + 20 PNGs
    reducer.ts         VfxAutoQuality, driven rather than read

Two instrument bugs are recorded here because both silently produced wrong
numbers before they were caught, and both are the kind that recur:

  * Headless rAF is vsync-locked, so every delta lands on a multiple of 16.7 ms
    and a 3 ms layer measures the same as a 15 ms one. The rig closes its clock
    with a one-pixel `readPixels` instead, which drains the GL pipeline.
  * A stage scaled to 1/2 is NOT a fill/geometry probe, however much it looks
    like one. A backdrop field is 2-5 screens across, so most of its geometry is
    off-viewport and clipped, and halving the stage pulls 4x more of it through
    the clipper. Measured, the star scenarios came out SLOWER at 1/2 (127 -> 157
    ms at 1280x720). Attribution is by scenario subtraction instead.

And one process note, since it cost a whole result: NEVER run a build or a test
suite while a timing rig is running. An "after" sweep read 183 ms where the
"before" read 133, entirely from a concurrent `vite build`. The clean A/B on a
quiet box says -60%.

1. THE NEBULA — CONFIRMED, and it is three quarters of the backdrop.
   3.0 to 6.1 blended screenfuls a frame, against the ground's 1.0 and the whole

   Worth flagging for the record: `NebulaSpec.overdraw` declares 2.175 for the

3. THE VOID'S OWN LAYERS — the ground is one opaque quad at 1.0x, the cheapest
   thing that can be in a frame, and it is not a blend. It stays.

   Does a resize rebake at the new size on the frame it happens? YES.
   `VoidBackdrop.configure` keys its rebuild on (boundsW, boundsH, viewW, viewH,
   density, nebula), so every distinct viewport during a window drag destroys and
   re-tessellates every layer — at 3440x1440 that is ~88,000 shapes rebuilt on a
   frame the player is already dragging. It is not the steady-state defect the

4. FULL-SCREEN THINGS THAT COULD BE CACHED — this is where the fix went. See 3
   and 4 below. Also checked and cleared: there are no PixiJS filters anywhere in
   the game render path (`grep -rn "filters ="` finds exactly one hit, in
   `src/ui/title-gate.ts`, which is a front screen). No full-screen post pass, no
   blur, no bloom pass. The overdraw table above is the whole story.

3. THE FINDING THE BRIEF DID NOT PREDICT — AN ULTRAWIDE PAID TWICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Look again at the sky column above. It is flat at 3.03-3.12 for every 16:9 row
and then jumps: 4.067 at 21:9 and 6.060 at 32:9. Overdraw is supposed to be
viewport-independent. It was not aspect-independent.

`mockup-reference.ts` `mockupBlobs` sized every element off the frame's WIDTH:

    const rx = screenW * pick(rng, sky.radius);

while element COUNT is per screen AREA (`mockupCount`: field area / screen area).
So a sky's coverage of the frame is

    count x pi k^2 screenW^2 / (screenW x screenH)  =  count x pi k^2 x W/H

— proportional to the aspect ratio, with nothing bounding it. The developer plays
on a 21:9 and owns a 32:9.

It is also, and this is the part that makes the fix a fidelity fix rather than a
perf hack, a LOOK drift. The design panel is 640x360 and the radius fractions
(0.18/0.38, 0.10/0.26, 0.16/0.26) are measured against its width. On a 32:9 frame
those same fractions make a clot twice the fraction of the frame's HEIGHT that
the design draws it at — so the sky reads coarser and emptier than the compositor
board the developer approved. One error, two faces.

THE FIX: `featureSpan(W, H) = sqrt(W x H x Wp/Hp)`.

It is the unique rule that satisfies both constraints:

  * IDENTITY AT THE DESIGN'S ASPECT. Put W/H = 16/9 in and it returns exactly W.
    Every number the design measured still means what it measured; no sky is
    re-art-directed to buy a millisecond, which is precisely the drift a0-40
    exists to undo. `backdrop-fill.test.ts` asserts this on the RULE, at four
    16:9 sizes and at the panel itself.
  * SHARE OF THE FRAME CONSTANT EVERYWHERE ELSE, because element area is
    proportional to span^2 and span^2 is proportional to W x H = the frame.

Rejected: the short side, `min(W, H)`. Scale-invariant too, but not the identity
at 16:9 — it would shrink every blob to 0.5625 of the design's on the very shape
the design was measured on. Re-art-directing the whole set to fix an ultrawide is
the wrong way round.

Measured after: the sky column reads 3.06 / 3.12 / 3.12 / 3.03 / 3.03 / 3.03
across the whole sweep. Flat. The three 16:9 rows are bit-identical to before.
3440x1440 total fill 26.7 -> 21.6 Mfrag/frame; 5120x1440 54.5 -> 32.2.

4. THE FIX AT THE LAYER THE PROFILE IMPLICATES — BAKE THE SKY ONCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The sky is three blended screenfuls a frame. It cannot be made cheaper by having
less of it: the counts, radii and alphas are `MOCKUP_REFERENCE`'s, a0-40 exists
because five briefs thinned them toward CI ceilings, and the ruling on this
backdrop is that the game matches the design — "not close enough, the same". So
the sky is drawn exactly as it is drawn, and what changes is WHERE.

`VoidBackdrop.configure` now wraps the sky's `Graphics` in a container and calls
`cacheAsTexture`. Two properties of this layer and only this layer make that
free, and both are load-bearing:

  1. IT ONLY EVER TRANSLATES. Geometry is played into a static `Graphics` at
     configure() and thereafter only `position` moves (`update`). Pixi's cached
     render group re-renders only when its CONTENTS change, and a parallax offset
     is not a change (RenderGroup.textureNeedsUpdate is set by init,
     enableCacheAsTexture and updateCacheTexture, and by nothing else). So the
     bake is once per (map, viewport, tier) — exactly where the geometry build
     already happens — and never in a frame.

  2. ITS SMALLEST FEATURE IS ENORMOUS. The smallest radius any sky declares is
     Patina Drift's 0.10 of featureSpan: 128 px at 1280x720, 297 px at 3440x1440.
     The alpha across it is a radial ramp. Linear interpolation error on (1-t^2)^2
     sampled every third pixel is (dt^2/8)*max|f''| = 5.5e-4 of peak alpha, and
     at Patina Drift's own peak (0.086) over its own colour delta (151 codes)
     that is 0.007 of ONE 8-bit code value.

Blend correctness is the thing that could have gone wrong silently, so it is
stated: the blend mode goes on BOTH the `Graphics` (so clots composite with each
other inside the cache exactly as they did on the frame buffer) and the wrapper
(so the finished cache composites onto the ground the same way). Source-over is
associative and additive is associative, so the two-step composite IS the
one-step composite. That is the whole licence for a cache to stand in for a
layer, and section 5 measures it rather than trusting it.

Resolution is derived from a MEMORY budget rather than set: `skyCacheResolution`
returns 1/3, or the first coarser whole fraction whose texture fits 2^21 texels
(2048x1024 = 8 MB at RGBA8) after the power-of-two rounding Pixi's texture pool
applies. 2048 is the WebGL2 GUARANTEED MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE, so a cache inside the
budget fits every device the game claims to run on, with no capability query and
no fallback path that only fires on hardware we do not own. Below 1/8 it declines
to cache and draws directly — correct and expensive, never absent, which is
r9-01's rule arriving in a new place.

The residual 3-6 code values are the ramp's DITHER resampling, not gradient
error — the mean difference is half a code value, and the error the interpolation
argument predicts is 0.007. And the banding measure moves the RIGHT way: the
largest single-pixel luma step along a scanline falls from 1.50-1.86 to
0.93-1.00, because bilinear magnification interpolates between quantised values.
The worry that a downsample would average a0-39's anti-banding noise into the
mush it exists to prevent was backwards: caching makes the sky SMOOTHER.

By eye: identical. The additive sky is the one that could have failed silently —
a wrong blend through a render target shows as a brighter or darker reef, or as a
rectangular seam where the texture ends — and Plasma Reef direct and cached are
indistinguishable, at the same peak Y' 59.8, with no seam. Iron Veil's rust band
and Coalsack's dust lane sit in the same places at the same values.

6. THE STATED FRAME BUDGET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    THE BACKDROP BLENDS AT MOST 2.5 SCREENFULS PER FRAME, AT ANY VIEWPORT, ON
    ANY MAP.

Enforced by `src/art/backdrop-fill.test.ts` on every push, over a six-viewport
sweep from the landscape phone to 32:9, on all six maps. It is stated on the
SHAPE of the stack rather than as a percentage: the ground is one opaque pass,
the sky is one textured pass, the star field is a third of a pass, total 2.331.
Anything above ~2.4 means a layer stopped being a single pass — a regression in
kind, which is what a gate should catch.

At the two sizes the brief asks about, and with the assumption stated so the
developer can substitute their own hardware's number:

    AT 3440x1440 (4.95 Mpx, the developer's screen)
      backdrop blends       11.5 Mfrag/frame     (was 26.7)
      at 60 fps             693 Mfrag/s          (was 1.60 Gfrag/s)
      on integrated graphics at a nominal 5 Gfrag/s of blended fill:
                            2.3 ms of a 16.7 ms frame   (was 5.3 ms)

    AT 5120x1440 (7.37 Mpx, 32:9)
      backdrop blends       17.2 Mfrag/frame     (was 54.5)
      at 60 fps             1.03 Gfrag/s         (was 3.27 Gfrag/s)
      same assumed rate:    3.4 ms of a 16.7 ms frame   (was 10.9 ms)

The 5 Gfrag/s is an assumption about integrated graphics, labelled as one; the
Mfrag/frame column is measured and portable. The shape of the before column is
the point: at 32:9 the backdrop alone wanted two thirds of a 60 fps frame for
nothing but the sky behind the game, and at 21:9 nearly a third. After, it wants
a seventh and a fifth.

WHAT THE FIX DOES *NOT* DO. It does not take the view away. Nothing about what a
desktop player sees changed — same viewport, same arena on screen, same sky, same
counts, same alphas, same brightness ladder. a0-74 is widening the mobile view
toward parity at the same time and this does not touch that trade; the developer
declined it once and it is not re-proposed here.

WHAT IS LEFT, IN ORDER, if this is not enough on the developer's box:

  a) `coverSpan` over-provisions every parallax field by a whole viewport — 4.9x
     the area. It costs no fill, and it costs 3.2x the star geometry baked and
     re-submitted every frame (the deep layer holds 49,378 shapes at 3440x1440
     where 15,560 would cover the screen) plus 4x the cache texture. See (b) in
     section 8: it is a Director's call, not a deferral.
  b) App-level MSAA. `main.ts:833` requests `antialias: true` on the
     `Application`, which is a 4x multiplier on the blended traffic of every pass
     including the backdrop's. It buys nothing on this content: every backdrop
     edge is the zero rim of a falloff where the alpha has already reached 0, and
     every entity is a `Sprite` off a texture the baker already antialiased. The
     one thing it does help is the arena wall's thin steel strokes. Platform
     Engineer's file, so it is raised and not changed.
  c) `resolution: window.devicePixelRatio` on the same call, uncapped, where the
     VFX texture cache caps at 2 for exactly this reason (main.ts:1477 — "a dpr-3
     phone would otherwise bake nine times the texels it can show"). On a HiDPI
     ultrawide, or with Windows display scaling at 125%/150%, the drawing buffer
     is 1.56x to 4x the CSS pixels — multiplying everything above. Also Platform
     Engineer's.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. VfxAutoQuality — IS IT ENGAGING, IS IT HELPING, SHOULD IT READ AREA?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Driven rather than read; full run in `reducer.txt`.

IS IT ENGAGING? Only at or below 30 fps sustained. 30 s of steady play:

     fps  frame ms  engaged  verdict
      25      40.0      yes  sheds VFX
      29      34.5      yes  sheds VFX
      30      33.3      yes  sheds VFX
      31      32.3       no  CHOPPY AND UNTOUCHED
      36      27.8       no  CHOPPY AND UNTOUCHED
      45      22.2       no  CHOPPY AND UNTOUCHED
      50      20.0       no  CHOPPY AND UNTOUCHED

Everything from 31 to 54 fps is a frame rate the player feels — a 40 fps frame on
a 60 Hz panel judders every third refresh — and the reducer never fires.

IS IT THRASHING? No, and the real failure is the opposite. A rate wandering +-8
fps around 26 fps engages 0% of the time over 60 s, because any excursion above
the floor resets `bandSeconds` to zero and a 4 s wobble crosses 30 twice a cycle.
It fires on a steady slog and not on a stutter, and a stutter is what a stutter
is. No chatter anywhere, at any centre: the 20 fps hysteresis gap and the two 3 s
sustain windows do exactly what their docstring promises.

SHOULD IT REACT TO VIEWPORT AREA RATHER THAN ONLY TO FRAME TIME? NO, and the
reason is what it can reach. Every lever the flag has is per-ENTITY — the
particle budget (`vfxField.quality`), impact glows, spawn shimmer, the station
halo — and r9-01 deliberately forbids it the sky mid-match, because the density
is pinned at build so a device that throttles at t+8 s keeps the sky it booted
with. a0-75 is a per-PIXEL cost. Wiring viewport area into this state machine
would have shed sparks on a big window while three screenfuls of sky went on
being paid for in full: the right signal reaching the wrong lever.

And after this branch there is nothing for an area input to react to. Backdrop
fill is 2.33 screenfuls at every viewport in the sweep and on every map. The
honest fix for a cost that scaled with area was to stop it scaling, not to detect
that it had.

One thing to hand to the Platform Engineer, since `vfx-quality.ts` is theirs:
`sample()` discards any frame over 1 s as a tab thaw (line 90). That is right for
a thaw, and it means a machine slow enough to produce genuinely 2 s frames —
which is what this container does at 5120x1440 — is invisible to the reducer. The
device that needs it most is the one it never sees. Not changed here; it is a
deliberate spike guard and weighing it is theirs.

8. SHOULD THE PERFORMANCE GATE SWEEP VIEWPORT SIZES?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yes, and it is worth being precise about why the studio's three existing gates
could not have caught this, rather than filing it as bad luck:

    tests/harness/perf.test.ts      profiles the SIM, which has no viewport
    tests/perf/draw-budget.spec.ts  counts draw calls and submitted entities,
                                    which barely move with the viewport
    tests/perf/frame-time.spec.ts   measures ONE fixed viewport, and gates only
                                    on hardware CI does not have

Every one is a real gate and none of them has an axis called AREA. So a per-pixel
cost that is fine at 1280x720, a third worse at 3440x1440 and double at 32:9 was
invisible to all three at once, and the developer found it by dragging a window
corner.

The art half of that axis is added here: `src/art/backdrop-fill.test.ts`, a unit
test rather than a browser suite ON PURPOSE. Fragments are countable without a
GPU, so the budget gates on every push instead of on a machine somebody has to
own, and it asserts the two things a millisecond cannot: that fill is FLAT across
aspect ratios, and that the sky layer is actually cached (an uncached sky is
pixel-identical and three screenfuls more expensive — the exact failure this
whole brief is about).

Two recommendations that are not art's to make, put here so they are on record:

  (a) `tests/perf/frame-time.spec.ts` should sweep, not sample. Two more
      viewports — a 21:9 and a 32:9 — would have turned a0-75 into a red gate
      before it reached the developer, and the whole cost of that is `page.
      setViewportSize`.
  (b) `coverSpan`'s over-provision (section 6a) needs a brief of its own. The
      arithmetic is now written on the function: the requirement is
      `max(view(1+f), view(1-f) + 2f*bound)` and it returns that plus a whole
      view AND the documented quarter-view of slack. The correction was
      implemented and measured on this branch and then REVERTED, because the field
      is not only a cover margin: a `lane` sky's `reach` and a `band` sky's spread
      are fractions of it, so shrinking it makes Coalsack's dust lane physically
      shorter and re-packs every structure that spans the field. Three ratified
      declarations went out of tolerance at once (Coalsack peak luma 46.3 -> 35.2,
      Patina Drift 32.3 -> 35.4, and a0-07b's own build-cost claim). Buying
      geometry by re-shaping three skies is the trade a0-40 exists to refuse. It
      wants a brief that re-derives the sky measurements against a corrected
      field. DIRECTOR'S CALL.
