ON WHETHER THIS RUN WAS TAKEN ON A QUIET BOX. It cannot be asserted — the studio
image is shared and no lane can see another's load. What CAN be said is that the
run is internally consistent: p95 sits within 13% of the median on the three
smallest viewports, and the fragment model below fits all six rows to 9% with a
single slope and a single intercept. A run being pushed around by a neighbour
does not usually fit a two-parameter model that well. That is why the before/
after comparison in section 9 of the audit is NOT taken across this run and a
later one, but by interleaving both bundles in one session (`ab-sweep.mjs`) —
this table's job is to establish the MODEL, not the baseline.

WHAT THE COLUMN SAYS
--------------------

`ms/Mpx` is NOT flat and it is not even monotonic — 435 → 253 → 201 → 185 → 219
→ 264. A pure pixel model does not describe this.

The fragment model does. Against the counted per-frame fill of the same scene
(`overdraw.txt`, before): 1.5 / 4.1 / 9.2 / 16.1 / 26.7 / 54.5 Mfrag.

  t = 93 ms + 34 ms × Mfrag

  viewport     Mfrag   predicted   measured   error
  ---------------------------------------------------
  phone          1.5       144.4      133.3    +8.3%
  1280×720       4.1       232.7      233.4    −0.3%
  1920×1080      9.2       406.4      416.7    −2.5%
  2560×1440     16.1       641.5      683.4    −6.1%
  3440×1440     26.7      1002.7     1083.3    −7.4%
  5120×1440     54.5      1949.3     1949.9    −0.0%

Every row inside 9%, with a 93 ms intercept that is everything in the frame that
is not backdrop fill (the entities, the HUD, the present) and a slope that is the
box's per-fragment tariff. The two rows the pixel model gets most wrong —
3440×1440 and 5120×1440 — are exactly the two where the sky's overdraw was
inflated by the aspect-ratio bug, and the fragment model absorbs them because it
counts that inflation.

**So: it is fill, and the backdrop's overdraw is the multiplier.** At 1280×720
the backdrop was 61% of the frame; at 5120×1440 it was 95%.
