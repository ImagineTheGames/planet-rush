3. SHOULD IT READ THE VIEWPORT? — no, and the reason is what it can reach

  What the flag actually sheds (main.ts:2570 and its callees):
    · vfxField.quality      — particle budget per burst (thins, never drops)
    · renderer.setReduceVfx — impact glows, spawn shimmer, station halo
    · VoidBackdrop          — NOTHING mid-match. r9-01 pins the sky density
                              at build on purpose, so a device that throttles
                              at t+8s keeps the sky it booted with.

  Every one of those is per-ENTITY. None of them is per-PIXEL, and a0-75 is
  a per-pixel cost: the backdrop was blending 5.40 screenfuls a frame at
  3440×1440 and 7.39 at 32:9. Wiring viewport area into this state machine
  would have shed sparks on a big window while the sky went on being paid
  for in full — a reducer reacting to the right signal with the wrong lever.

  And after the fix there is nothing left for an area input to react to.
  Backdrop fill is now 2.33 screenfuls at EVERY viewport in the sweep and on
  every map (src/art/backdrop-fill.test.ts). The honest fix for a cost that
  scaled with area was to stop it scaling, not to detect that it had.

  One thing worth handing to the Platform Engineer, since it is their file:
  `sample()` discards any frame over 1 s (vfx-quality.ts:90) as a tab thaw.
  That is right for a thaw and it means a genuinely 2 s frame — which is what
  the container measures at 5120×1440 — is invisible to the reducer, so a
  machine slow enough to need it most is the one it never sees. Not changed
  here: it is a deliberate spike guard and moving it is theirs to weigh.
