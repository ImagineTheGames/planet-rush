a0-53 — the star bloom, measured in rendered pixels
====================================================================

Every number is measured off a committed frame in ./frames, at the camera
offset the client published: (-1328, -800). The design side is
haloRadiusOf(BLOOM.intensity) — the preview's own rad*(5+13*inten) — scaled
by the radius of the very star being measured, so neither side of the ratio
is a constant agreeing with itself.

THE DESIGN
----------
  BLOOM.intensity            0.48
  haloRadiusOf(intensity)    11.24   = 5 + 13 x 0.48
  BLOOM.radius               11.24   (agrees; NOT moved by this brief)
  haloPeakAlphaOf(intensity) 0.20159999999999997   = 0.42 x 0.48
  starHaloAlpha()            0.2016
  halo curve                 three stops, knee 0.3095 of peak at t=0.35
  star radius range          0.4 .. 2.45 px
  bloom threshold            magnitude > 0.86
  => a bloomed star's own r  (2.1630, 2.45]
  => its halo radius         (24.31, 27.54] px
  spike arm                  6.9688 x r  = 0.62 of the halo

  The design gradient, as a fraction of its peak (haloProfile):
    t         0.10   0.20   0.35   0.50   0.62   0.75   0.90   1.00
    value    0.803  0.605  0.310  0.238  0.181  0.119  0.048  0.000

  A ratio ABOVE 1 in the shipped table is not a wide halo: it is a star whose
  surroundings are world content (a rock edge, a station arc) that the clean-
  sky filter did not catch, so its light never comes back to ground. They are
  left in rather than hand-dropped; the median is what the gate reads, and the
  five uncontaminated stars agree with it at 0.666-0.695.

  What that last row costs the measurement: past t=0.9 the design itself
  carries under 5% of its peak, which over Floor is under one 8-bit code
  value. A CORRECTLY drawn halo therefore measures a few per cent short of
  its geometry, and every ratio below is read with that floor in mind.
