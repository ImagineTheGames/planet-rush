1. HOW MUCH WORLD YOU CAN SEE  (report 1)
-----------------------------------------
The developer:

  "on pc i have the entire screen but im on mobile im confined to a very small
   portion of the world. perhaps we need a way to either confine the screen on pc
   or what i'd probably prefer is add a zoom out button on mobile"

THE CAUSE, IN ONE LINE: the camera was translate-only, so ONE WORLD UNIT WAS ONE
CSS PIXEL. The slice of the claim a player saw was exactly the pixel size of the
glass they were holding. Nobody chose that ratio; it fell out of the hardware.
(`src/platform/camera.ts` before this branch; `WORLD_SIZE` = 2400.)

MEASURED, BEFORE (zoom 1x — the shipped camera, every profile):

  profile                      viewport     world units   vs desktop   of arena
  ---------------------------  -----------  ------------  -----------  --------
  desktop 1707x898 (developer) 1707 x  898  1707 x  898       100.0%     71.1%
  desktop 1280x800 (goldens)   1280 x  800  1280 x  800        75.0%     53.3%
  phone landscape 798x384      798  x  384   798 x  384        46.7%     33.3%
  phone landscape 844x390      844  x  390   844 x  390        49.4%     35.2%
  phone PORTRAIT held 390x844  844  x  390   844 x  390        49.4%     35.2%

  THE RATIO THE COMPLAINT IS: 798 / 1707 = 0.467. A phone player saw 46.7% of
  the world width a desktop player saw — 33% of the arena against 71%.

  The portrait row is not a typo and it matters: the game is landscape-locked
  (`orientation.ts`), so a phone held upright lays out at 844x390 exactly as one
  held sideways. There is no third case; portrait is the landscape case rotated.

MEASURED, AFTER (the touch zoom-out control, walking its ladder through the same
`setViewZoom` a real tap runs):

  profile                  rung   world units    vs desktop 1707   of arena
  -----------------------  -----  -------------  ----------------  --------
  phone landscape 798x384  1x      798 x  384          46.7%         33.3%
  phone landscape 798x384  1.5x   1197 x  576          70.1%         49.9%
  phone landscape 798x384  2x     1596 x  768          93.5%         66.5%
  phone landscape 844x390  1x      844 x  390          49.4%         35.2%
  phone landscape 844x390  1.5x   1266 x  585          74.2%         52.8%
  phone landscape 844x390  2x     1688 x  780          98.9%         70.3%
  phone PORTRAIT   390x844 2x     1688 x  780          98.9%         70.3%

  THE GAP CLOSES. At the top rung the smaller phone reaches 93.5% of the
  developer's desktop view and the larger one 98.9% — and both exceed a
  1280-wide desktop window (1280 units) outright. It is not left half-closed and
  there is nothing here for the developer to have to rule on.

WHY THE LADDER STOPS AT 2x, and it is a measurement rather than a preference:
  - 2x already reaches 93.5% / 98.9%. The remaining 6.5% on the smaller phone is
    worth less than what buying it costs.
  - `SHIP_RADIUS` is 16 world units. At 1x a ship is 32 CSS px across; at 2x it
    is 16; a 3x rung would draw it at 10.7 and a hull bar under it thinner than
    the bar's own stroke. The frames in ./shots/after-phone-zoom-2x.png are the
    check on that: at 2x the station is still a readable object and the whole
    asteroid field plus the arena wall are on screen.

WHAT WAS **NOT** DONE, and why (the brief is explicit): the desktop view is NOT
confined. That is the other option the developer named and did not choose, and it
takes something away from every existing player. Desktop is untouched — the
control is drawn on touch only, and `cameraScale(1)` is byte-for-byte the camera
that shipped (asserted in src/ui/viewport.test.ts).

DEFAULT: the ladder starts at 1x — the shipped view — and the player's choice is
remembered across matches (`planet-rush:viewZoom`, the same storage seam as the
fire mode and the control scheme). FOR THE DEVELOPER TO RULE ON: whether the
first-run default on touch should be 1.5x rather than 1x. A button that defaults
to off leaves a fresh phone player at 46.7% until they find it; a default of 1.5x
would seat every phone at 70.1% with the shipped view one tap away. Both rungs
are measured above. Not decided here, because "what the game looks like on first
boot" is not a placement fix.


2. THE HUD ON AN ULTRAWIDE  (report 2)
--------------------------------------
  "on pc we also need a way to handle UI locations because i have an ultra wide
   and all that UI goes to the edges of the screens"

THE FIX: the HUD's chrome is bound to a CONTENT BOX — a centred region of the
HUD's own reference aspect — and the world renders full-bleed behind it.

WHY 16:9 IS THE CAP, and why it is not a taste: `src/ui/instrument.ts` scales
every HUD metric from `HUD_REFERENCE = 1280x720`, which is 16:9 exactly. Binding
the chrome to that aspect gives an ultrawide the HUD *as it was authored*, scaled
— rather than the same HUD pulled apart until the build wheel and the hold pips
are a head-turn from each other. Anything wider is world, which is what an
ultrawide is for.

WHY THERE IS ALSO A FLOOR, and why it is not optional: a bare aspect cap gets
phones badly wrong. A 844x390 landscape phone is aspect **2.16** — already wider
than 16:9 — so a pure cap would inset the one device with no horizontal room to
give away. The box is `min(W, max(FLOOR, H x 16/9))` with FLOOR =
`HUD_REFERENCE.width` (1280): under the frame the HUD was drawn for, nothing is
ever inset. Height is never capped — all three reports are horizontal, and an
ultrawide is short rather than tall.

MEASURED (read off the client; `content` is the box, `world` is what the camera
shows, and the two differing IS the full-bleed claim):

  profile                viewport      content box        world on screen
  ---------------------  ------------  -----------------  ----------------
  16:9  1920x1080        1920 x 1080   x=0    w=1920      1920 x 1080
  21:9  2560x1080        2560 x 1080   x=320  w=1920      2560 x 1080
  32:9  3840x1080        3840 x 1080   x=960  w=1920      3840 x 1080
  desktop 1707x898       1707 x  898   x=55   w=1596      1707 x  898
  desktop 1280x800       1280 x  800   x=0    w=1280      1280 x  800
  phone landscape 844x390 844 x  390   x=0    w=844        844 x  390
  phone landscape 798x384 798 x  384   x=0    w=798        798 x  384

  The world column never shrinks. The box only ever narrows, and only on a
  display past BOTH the reference aspect and the floor: 21:9 and 32:9 lose 320
  and 960 px per side to the world, the developer's own 1707x898 window loses 55,
  and every phone and every 16:9-or-narrower window loses nothing at all.

SCREENSHOTS — ./shots/, all three aspects the DoD names, before and after, same
frozen scene, same height (1080) so the box is the same 1920 px in all three and
the only difference between frames is how much world is either side of it:

  before-fit-16x9-1920x1080.png   after-fit-16x9-1920x1080.png   (the control)
  before-fit-21x9-2560x1080.png   after-fit-21x9-2560x1080.png
  before-fit-32x9-3840x1080.png   after-fit-32x9-3840x1080.png

  Read at full size, every one. What they show:
  - 16:9 — IDENTICAL before and after, and that is the point of shooting it: the
    box is the viewport there, so ORE is at x=16, HOME's bar ends at 1904 and the
    minimap is in the true corner, exactly as they always were.
  - 21:9 — before: ORE at the far left edge, HOME at the far right, 2528 px
    apart. After: 336 and 2224, so 1888 px apart, both inside the centred box.
  - 32:9 — before: ORE at x=10 and HOME's right edge at 3830, **3820 px apart**,
    with the minimap alone in a corner nearly a metre of desk away from the build
    wheel. After: 976 and 2864. The world behind them is untouched in both — the
    star field, the rocks and the enemy station out at x=290 fill all 3840 px in
    the "after" frame too, which is the full-bleed half of the claim.

WHAT IS BOUND: the ore readout, the wave clock, the HOME cluster, the controls
strip and its scrim, the minimap (and its hit test, from the same offset), the
onboarding prompt's band and wrap, the respawn overlay's wrap, and the alarm
frame — a threat-red frame on the physical edge of a 32:9 is a frame drawn for
nobody.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT BOUND, and this is the load-bearing half: everything
that tracks the WORLD rather than the screen — over-ship health bars, nameplates,
the under-ship hold pips, Tap Commander's order markers, the loot tell. Those
follow the camera, and binding them to a box would tear them off the thing they
label. Visible in the after 32:9 frame: the enemy nameplates out at x=290 and
x=580, correctly outside the box, sitting on the ships they name.

THE SCREEN-EDGE ARROW asks two questions of two rectangles, deliberately: "is
home already on screen" of the WHOLE viewport (the world is full-bleed, so a
station in the gutter is a station you can see, and GDD §2.2 says the station is
then the tell), and "where does the arrow go" of the CONTENT BOX (an arrow is
only a tell if it is read).

TEST: `the HUD stays reachable on an ultrawide` in src/ui/hud.test.ts asserts the
anchors sit inside the box at 32:9 — named individually, so a bug that piled them
all in the middle would fail — and that every one clears the physical edge by the
960 px gutter. Two companion tests pin that 16:9 and every phone are unchanged,
and that 21:9 binds by the same rule rather than as a special case.


3. CONTROLS THE SEATED SCHEME CANNOT USE  (report 3)
----------------------------------------------------
  "theres buttons like Fire, that shouldn't show up since im using tap commander
   and auto fire (on pc)"

"on pc" IS NOT A MISTAKE, and it is the finding under the finding. `src/main.ts`
seats the touch layer on `'ontouchstart' in window || navigator.maxTouchPoints > 0`,
which is TRUE on any touchscreen laptop. So a developer on a PC gets the on-glass
thumb furniture, and this report is about that — not about the desktop controls
strip, which has been scheme-aware since a0-37 and already drops its fire row
under Tap Commander.

WHAT I FOUND IN THAT CATEGORY, read off `sampleInput` rather than guessed from
the buttons' names. Under `scheme === 'tap'` it runs, every frame:

    merged.thrust.x = 0; merged.thrust.y = 0;
    merged.aim = null;   merged.fire = false;
    // ...and TapPilot writes all three from the standing order.

  HIDDEN (all three, on desktop and on touch alike):
    - the hold-to-FIRE button          — the one reported
    - the LEFT THRUST STICK (ghost + live base + knob)
    - the RIGHT AIM STICK (ghost + live base + knob)
  Every one of them writes into a field discarded on the next line. The engaged
  halves are gated as well as the idle ghosts: a scheme that cannot use a stick
  must not draw one under a thumb either, however that thumb got there.

  KEPT, and hiding it would be the same defect pointing the other way:
    - BUILD & UPGRADE — `merged.build` is deliberately NOT zeroed there, so that
      button really does open the wheel under Tap Commander.
    - the minimap toggle, the pause button — live in both schemes.
    - the desktop controls strip — already correct (a0-37); under Tap Commander
      it lists `Click anywhere / Move or attack` and the Build row, and nothing
      else. Re-checked, not re-fixed.

  FOUND AND DELIBERATELY KEPT — reported rather than acted on:
    - The SETTINGS "FIRE MODE" row. It is genuinely inert while Tap Commander is
      seated: `sampleInput` derives the mode from the pilot's lock
      (`tapPilot.lockedRef ? Manual : AutoAim`) and ignores the player's setting
      entirely. But it configures the OTHER scheme, and hiding a setting because
      the current scheme ignores it would strand a player who wants to switch
      back. A dead BUTTON on the play surface and a currently-irrelevant SETTINGS
      ROW are different things; only the first is what the report is about.

  MEASURED AND NOT DONE, stated so it is not mistaken for missed: the prompt
  band still reserves `PROMPT_THUMB_COLUMN` (156 px each side) and the minimap
  still holds `MINIMAP_FIRE_COLUMN` (120 px) clear of a FIRE button that, since
  a0-30 made Tap Commander the default everywhere, is usually not drawn at all.
  That is ~430 px of a 844 px phone reserved for furniture that is not there —
  real, and worth a brief. It is a LAYOUT change, not a dead-control one, and it
  moves every phone golden, so it is reported here rather than smuggled in.

FRAMES: ./shots/before-phone-zoom-1x.png against ./shots/after-phone-zoom-1x.png,
same phone, same frozen scene, both seated in the shipped defaults (Tap Commander
+ Auto-aim). Before: a full-size FIRE button in the bottom-right and the left
thrust-stick ghost in the bottom-left, neither of which moves the ship. After:
both gone, and the VIEW control in the top-right in their place.

TEST: `draws no FIRE button under Tap Commander + auto-fire` in
src/ui/live-controls.test.ts — the developer's case by name, plus the two sticks
that go with it, plus a test that the sticks scheme is left exactly as it shipped.


4. THE CONTROL ITSELF
---------------------
VIEW 1x / 1.5x / 2x, cycled by one tap, top-right under the HOME cluster,
touch-only, persisted. Drawn rect, measured on the client: 64x48 at (718, 61) on
the 798-wide phone and (764, 61) on the 844-wide one.

WHERE IT SITS WAS MEASURED, NOT PICKED. The first attempt was the same ROW as
HOME and the 568x320 profile killed it: at the 0.75 scale floor the wave clock's
strip is ~151 px wide centred on 284 (so it runs to ~360) and HOME's left edge is
at 398 — 38 px of gap for a 64 px control. Stacked UNDER HOME it fits, but only
with a clamp: the `top-right` anchor zone is y in [16, 106.7] there, 90 px tall,
and HOME's own chrome takes 39 of it. So the control gives up its GAP and never
its SIZE — a HUD metric scales with the frame, a touch target does not, because a
thumb does not get smaller with the viewport (GDD §2.4). 48 px, `TOUCH_TARGET_MIN`,
on every profile. src/ui/zoom-control.test.ts pins all of it against the layout
registry's own resolver at five profiles.


5. WHAT ELSE THIS TOUCHED, AND WHAT IT DID NOT
----------------------------------------------
CROSS-LANE, both marked as their own commits and both additive-with-defaults, so
either drops cleanly if its owner declines:
  - src/platform/camera.ts + src/render/{index,cull}.ts — the camera scale. No
    control in src/ui can close report 1 without a scale to turn, and
    `src/render/cull.ts` said in so many words "if a zoom is ever added, this
    file is where it lands". `cameraOffset(t, vp, cameraScale(1))` is asserted
    equal to `cameraOffset(t, vp)`.
  - src/platform/touch-visuals.ts — one `sticksLive = true` argument. The
    DECISION stays in src/ui/live-controls.ts; the view is handed a boolean.

NOT RE-BAKED, NOT RE-POOLED: every entity texture is rasterised at its own fixed
pixels-per-unit and drawn at `radius / ART_SCALE`. A camera scale under 1 only
minifies what is already minified, so the atlas, the sprite pool and the draw-call
count are untouched. What does change on a zoomed-out phone is how many bodies
clear the cull — `writeVisibleWorld` now divides by the scale, so at 2x a phone
submits roughly what a desktop already submits today. That is the same budget the
desktop has been meeting since a1-12, on the device with less headroom, and it is
opt-in.

THE SIM IS UNTOUCHED. The zoom is a render transform: no constant, no rule and no
action changed, and both camera inverses in main.ts (`logicalToWorld`,
`worldToLogical`) take the scale — without that a Tap Commander order placed while
zoomed out would land at a fraction of the distance the player pointed at.
