a0-72 — "the server would not take me back", and it should have
================================================================================
OWNER: Netcode Engineer.  Branch: agent/netcode/a0-72-rejoin-always.
Captured 2026-08-17 against the local fleet fixture (tests/net/local-fleet.ts):
two ticket-enforcing Machines with the socket-hop pin armed, a Fly-shaped edge in
front of them, a real allocator process — and the shipped client path
(allocateRoom -> allocatorTransport -> createOnlineSession -> WebSocketTransport).

The report
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  "on mobile i got disconnected because the screen went black, and when i went
   back i saw refused - the server would not take you back... that doesnt feel
   right to me, i should be able to join back if the match is still on-going no
   matter what"

The last sentence is the ruling this audit is measured against.


HOW IT WAS REPRODUCED (and why not by killing the socket)
================================================================================
A killed socket fires `onclose`, the transport redials inside a second, and the
reclaim lands long before anything can lapse. That is tests/net/reconnect-resume.
test.ts, and it was green throughout the whole of this bug. A BACKGROUNDED PAGE
runs no handlers at all: the screen blanks, the tab is frozen, the radio drops
the TCP connection, and the client learns none of it — `transport.state` still
reads `open`. Whatever it does next happens N seconds later, on the player's
return, and N is the entire failure.

  evidence/a0-72-rejoin/rejoin-probe.ts

freezes the page (a socket that stops calling the page back and then dies), waits
N REAL seconds — nothing here moves a clock, because the two windows under test
are measured by the allocator and the server in real seconds — and then calls
`session.linkShown()`, which is exactly what `visibilitychange` does in the
shipped client.

  npx vite-node evidence/a0-72-rejoin/rejoin-probe.ts -- --away=35000 --label=X
  npx vite-node evidence/a0-72-rejoin/rejoin-probe.ts -- --away=35000 --end-match=1 --label=Y


1. THE REFUSAL, NAMED
================================================================================
  before-away-35s.json   (commit 5388c0f, i.e. BEFORE any fix)

  allocate:  { room: KFKW, machine: 4d891e33b27a95, expiresInMs: 30014 }
  away:      35.0 s
  frames:    ... snapshot, snapshot, [35242 ms] joinError { reason: "bad-ticket" }

  refusal:   closeReason      "join-rejected"
             rejectReason     "bad-ticket"
             transportState   "closed"

  trace (the same one DOWNLOAD LOG hands the developer):
             allocating ALLOCATING ROOM...
             ticketed   ROOM KFKW - TICKET SIGNED
             dialing    DIALING MACHINE 4d891e33...
             joined     JOINED - SEAT 1
             refused    REFUSED: bad-ticket - machine mismatch

  overlay:   "REFUSED - the server would not take you back"
             "Nothing left to reclaim - the reconnect window closed while you
              were away."

  AND, AT THE SAME INSTANT, ON THE SERVER:
             roomStillHosted  true
             matchPhase       "live"
             pendingReclaim   true      <-- the seat was BEING HELD
             botFlyingIt      true
             ship.banked      40, tier 3 on the ship they were flying

So: **the ticket expired, and nothing else.** The seat was there. The match was
running. 25 seconds of the 60-second reconnect grace were still unspent. What
refused the player was the allocator's 30-second routing pass
(allocator/allocator.ts DEFAULT_TICKET_TTL_MS = 30_000), which the transport
minted once for the first dial and re-presented verbatim on every redial for the
rest of the session — so a screen that stays off for longer than half a minute
comes back holding a pass that lapsed while its owner was away.

Two further untruths in that capture, both worth naming:

  * the overlay's detail line said the reconnect window had closed. It had not.
  * the trace glossed `bad-ticket` as "machine mismatch". There was no machine
    mismatch; the ticket named 4d891e33b27a95 and the room was on
    4d891e33b27a95. The gloss was standing in front of an expiry.

THE OTHER TWO WALLS, for the record. The ticket is simply the first one a phone
hits; behind it stood two more, both of which would have refused a player who got
past it:

  2. the reconnect grace — server/room.ts held a dropped seat for 60 s and then
     gave it to the bot for good (`reclaim-expired`), and src/net/reconnect.ts
     stopped the client redialling at the same 60 s (`grace-elapsed`);
  3. the sweep — server/match-server.ts deleted any room with no connected human
     and no pending reclaim, so 60 s after the last human dropped the room the
     code named was gone (`reclaim-unknown`).

A phone backgrounding is the ordinary case, not an edge case, and every one of
those three numbers is small enough to refuse a returning player routinely.

3. THE REFUSAL THAT REMAINS, SAYING SOMETHING TRUE
================================================================================
  after-match-over.json — page backgrounded, and the match ENDS while they are
  away. There is genuinely nothing to come back to, and that is now its own word
  rather than the window's:

  refusal:   rejectReason "match-over"
  trace:     refused    REFUSED: match-over - that match has finished
  overlay:   "MATCH OVER - it finished while you were away"
             "Your seat was held the whole time the match ran - the match is what
              ended, not your window."

Every other surviving refusal is named the same way (src/net/link-loss.ts
`refusalTitle` / `refusalDetail`, asserted in src/net/session.test.ts):

  reclaim-unknown  MATCH ENDED - that room is no longer running
  reclaim-denied   SEAT TAKEN - somebody else is flying it
  reclaim-expired  SEAT RELEASED - you left this match, so it went on without you
  match-live       MATCH UNDER WAY - it started without you
  room-full        ROOM FULL - every seat is taken
  bad-ticket       WRONG SERVER - your pass is for a different machine
  bad-room-code    NOT A ROOM CODE - check the letters

"REFUSED - the server would not take you back" is kept for exactly one case: a
reason this build has never heard of, where the token is printed rather than
explained by guesswork.


4. WHAT CHANGED, AND WHAT EACH ARTEFACT PROVES
================================================================================
  THE PASS   src/net/websocket-transport.ts `refreshTicket` re-mints the ticket
             before every post-drop dial (src/net/session.ts `allocatorTransport`
             wires it to POST /rooms/:code/join). The TTL stays 30 s — a leaked
             pass must still go stale — so the pass is re-minted, not stretched.
             ALSO server/match-server.ts `admitsJoin(code, ticket, reclaiming)`:
             a Machine ALREADY HOSTING the room admits a reclaim on a
             lapsed-but-genuine ticket, because the ticket answers "which
             Machine" (checkable against its own room table) while the
             `reclaimToken` answers "is this seat yours". Conflating those two
             questions was the bug. Forged / tampered / wrong-room / wrong-Machine
             tickets are refused exactly as before, and so is a lapsed pass on a
             FRESH join, and so is a lapsed pass into a room this Machine does not
             host.
             PROVEN BY: after-away-35s.json (end to end), and by
             src/net/session.test.ts "lets a returning owner in on a lapsed
             ticket, with no allocator to ask" — which fails with
             `[ 'bad-ticket' ]` the moment the exception is removed (verified by
             reverting it and re-running).

  THE WINDOW server/room.ts `HELD_FOR_MATCH`: a mid-match drop is not on a clock.
             The seat is released when the match ENDS, or the instant the player
             says ABANDON. Client-side the same rule: src/net/reconnect.ts retries
             a live room however long we were away, and src/net/link-loss.ts
             `holdForMatch()` (called on `matchStart`) stops the watchdog ending a
             match on its own arithmetic — that arithmetic was always an estimate,
             and an estimate that ends a match is a client refusing a seat the
             server is holding open.
             PROVEN BY: after-away-90s.json, and tests/server/match-server.test.ts
             "holds the seat for the whole match, however long the player is away".

  THE SWEEP  A live match with a reclaimable seat is never garbage. What bounds
             the room instead is the match itself: at `matchEnd` the held seats
             fall back to `graceMs`, whose one remaining job is keeping a finished
             room alive long enough to answer `match-over` before it is swept.
             PROVEN BY: after-match-over.json, and tests/server/match-server.test.ts
             "sweeps a room once the match is over and nobody may come back".

  THE WORDS  src/net/link-loss.ts `refusalTitle` / `refusalDetail`, and the trace
             gloss in src/net/connect-trace.ts.
             PROVEN BY: after-match-over.json and src/net/session.test.ts
             "says something true and specific when the match really is over".


5. THE COST, STATED
================================================================================
Holding a seat for the life of the match is not free, and the bill is NOT the one
that first looks likely:

  * it is NOT seat availability. A live match already refuses new joins
    (`joinableSeats` is 0 once the phase is not `lobby`, and `join` answers
    `match-live`), so a ghost seat was never a seat anybody else could have taken.
    A 6-seat room with three ghosts has three bots flying, which is what it had
    before this change too — the difference is only that the three seats stay
    reclaimable instead of becoming permanently a bot's after 60 s.

  * it IS server residency. Before this change, a room whose only human dropped
    died 60 seconds later. Now it lives to the end of the match, so a Machine
    cannot idle down while a fully-ghosted match plays itself out. That is
    bounded — src/sim/match.ts `collapseDeadline` forces an ending, and GDD §1
    targets 10-15 minutes — so the worst case is one room-worth of CPU
    (~4.7 ms/s measured, docs/server-capacity.md) for the rest of one match.

No limit is imposed here, deliberately: the ruling says "no matter what", and the
brief says a limit is the developer's to pick. The design question is in the PR
body, with numbers.
