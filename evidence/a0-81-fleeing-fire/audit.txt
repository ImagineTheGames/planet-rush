a0-81 — a bot running home should still be shooting at whoever is chasing it
Bot Engineer · branch agent/bots/a0-81-fire-while-fleeing · 2026-08-17

The developer, from a live match:

  "when rusty was fleeing from me he could have auto fired at me but instead he
   didnt it was like he was in a retreat or return to base target but thats dumb
   because its unfair for him to just target his base and not fire at me at same
   time because thats what i would do"


===============================================================================
1. THE AUDIT — which of the two it was
===============================================================================

The brief offered two hypotheses. It is the SECOND, and it is starker than
"gated": the fire decision was not suppressed by the movement state, it was
hard-coded off in the movement branch.

NOT one target field doing two jobs.
  `retreat(ctx, threat)` takes the threat it is running from as an ARGUMENT and
  reads the destination separately off `ctx.self.station`. `engage`/`attack`
  take a position or a `TargetScore`. There is no shared `target` slot on
  `Brain` (src/bots/tree.ts) that a destination and an aim point are both
  written into — checked field by field. The two intents were already separate
  data; what was missing was one of them.

A fire intent nailed shut by the travelling branch.
  Every branch whose job was "fly somewhere" ended with a literal `fire(false)`
  and NO `aim` action at all:

    src/bots/behaviors.ts  L1602/1604/1605   retreat        (all three exits)
    src/bots/behaviors.ts  L661             haulHome        (= homeErrand)
    src/bots/behaviors.ts  L600             spendAtHome     (docked at the wheel)
    src/bots/behaviors.ts  L684             defendHome      (empty guard ring)
    src/bots/behaviors.ts  L1106            defendAlly      (transit leg)

  It was deliberate, and the comment said so. `retreat`'s doc block: "with a
  live threat the weapon is off — a retreating bot does not advertise (GDD
  §2.2)." So this is a ratified design line being overridden by the developer,
  not an oversight being repaired, and the PR says so.

Why it looked broken rather than merely passive.
  The missing `aim` is the half that made it read as a bug from the cockpit.
  With no aim action the simulation's facing ladder (src/sim/step.ts
  `desiredFacing`) falls through to priority 3, "nose follows travel" — so a
  retreating bot was not holding fire while looking at you, it was pointed at
  its own doorstep with its back to the chase. That is exactly the developer's
  "it was like he was in a retreat or return to base target".

Why firing over the shoulder genuinely costs the bot nothing.
  src/sim/step.ts `integrate` applies `intent.thrust` in WORLD space and never
  reads `ship.angle`; facing steers only the gun (`fireShip` fires along
  `ship.angle`). A hull thrusting home with its nose over its shoulder gets home
  at exactly the speed it always did. The developer's "it costs them nothing" is
  the simulation's own arithmetic, which is why they expect it to happen.


===============================================================================
2. THE MEASUREMENT — a chased bot's shots while retreating, per tier
===============================================================================

Instrument: evidence/a0-81-fleeing-fire/audit.ts
  npx vite-node evidence/a0-81-fleeing-fire/audit.ts

Eight full 8-slot FFA matches, the shipped cast (rusty, bolt, foreman, patch,
sable, vulture, warden), 300 sim-seconds each, seeds
1,7,23,37,101,991,20260806,20260817. No fixtures and no staging: the bots pick
their own fights, take their own damage, and break off when their own nerve says
to. Both builds run the identical script.

  retreat tick   a tick whose winning behavior-tree leaf was `retreat`
                 (Brain.lastBehavior) — the bot's own account, not an inference
                 from its velocity.
  chased tick    a retreat tick with a hostile ship inside WEAPON_RANGE at the
                 same moment. This is the only denominator the complaint is
                 about; a bot retreating across empty space has nothing to shoot
                 at and counting those ticks dilutes both builds toward zero.
  shot           a projectile that actually left the barrel, detected from the
                 weapon reload jumping back to full inside the step
                 (`fireWeapon`) — so a held trigger on cooldown counts once,
                 when the shot goes out, exactly like a human's.

--- AFTER (this branch) -------------------------------------------------------

easy             24         202420         22395           6557     500              1.34          4679

Read it as: across eight matches and 64 bot-seats, a bot was chased while
retreating for **58,258 ticks (16 minutes of sim time)** and fired **zero**
shots. Not "rarely", not "badly" — no bot in the game could do it at all, at any
tier, which is what the developer met. After, every tier does.

The ladder, which is the thing that had to survive:

  tier    trigger held / chased tick   shots per chased second
  easy              29%                        1.34
  medium            59%                        2.16
  hard              75%                        2.32

Easy is a third as willing to have the gun on target as Hard and lands 42% fewer
shots per second of being chased — and there is no new knob anywhere. That
spread is the aim-error model the tier ladder is already built on doing its job
on a new behaviour: `aimJitter` (0.32 rad at Easy vs ~0.02 at Hard) sprays the
committed bearing so the hull rarely settles on it, `aimLatency` leads a juking
chaser where it WAS going, and `reactionInterval` (1/6 s vs 1/24 s) refreshes
the whole aim four times less often while the geometry of a chase changes every
metre.


===============================================================================
3. THE DIFFICULTY DECISION — every tier, not Medium-and-up
===============================================================================

The brief invited a proposal to gate this at Medium. Rejected, for one decisive
reason and one principled one.

Decisive: the bot in the report is RUSTY, and Rusty is Easy (GDD §2.9 — "timid
hoarder"). A Medium-and-up rule ships the developer's own match back to them
unchanged. The brief itself draws the line: "Easy being bad at it is different
from *no bot in the game being able to do it at all*, which is what the developer
met." Section 2 is that sentence measured — 0.00 at every tier.

Principled: this codebase already has the mechanism for making Easy bad at a
thing, and it refuses to add a second one. The radio note (src/bots/ally.ts,
docs/team-bots-plan.md §4.5) states the doctrine outright — "Cooperation is a
difficulty dial expressed through the existing latency and miss model — there is
no separate 'be bad at teamwork' knob." Covering fire uses the identical
`aimAndFire` as `engage`, with the same `combatSpread`, the same
`combatLatency`, and the same aim track, so it inherits the whole ladder for
free and cannot become a second, quietly better gun. The measured 29/59/75%
spread in section 2 is that decision validated rather than asserted.


===============================================================================
4. WHERE COVERING FIRE APPLIES — and where it deliberately does not
===============================================================================

Applied to the branches that sit ABOVE the combat branches in all three trees:

    retreat · haulHome/homeErrand · spendAtHome · defendHome's idle ring ·
    defendAlly's transit leg

Reaching one of those means the bot was denied a fight by an OUTRANKING
COMMITMENT, and a commitment is a reason not to fly at someone, never a reason
not to shoot them.

NOT applied to `roam`, `scavenge`, `hunt`. Those sit BELOW `potshot`/`attack` in
every tree, so a bot only reaches them once its own tier declined the fight —
Easy's `potshot` demands `weights.triangle.attack >= EASY_ATTACK_WEIGHT` (0.4)
before Rusty will shoot at something already in front of it. Arming them would
hand the Easy tree the seek-and-destroy branch GDD §2.9 withholds from it
("attacks rarely"): a balance change wearing a bug fix's clothes. The defect was
a suppressed trigger, not a missing appetite. Pinned by the test
`leaves roam and scavenge deliberately unarmed`.

What did NOT change: a bot with nothing hostile inside weapon range emits the
same released trigger and no aim action it always did (`TRIGGER_UP`), so the
half of "gunfire is the loudest tell" (GDD §2.2) that is load-bearing — a bot
crossing empty space is quiet — is untouched. And an ally is never covering
fire, at any range, in any tier: `coveringFireTarget` asks `isTargetable`, the
one `hostile` stamp off sim/allegiance.ts, tested with two slots sharing a side
because FFA cannot prove it (GDD §2.9).


===============================================================================
5. THE MIRROR CASE — does combat suppress economic decisions the same way?
===============================================================================

Asked by the brief. Answer: NOT the same defect, with one half that WAS and is
now fixed.

(a) Half of it was the same defect, and it is fixed here.
    `spendAtHome` hard-coded `fire(false)` while the bot orbited its own dock
    with the wheel open — a bot banking or buying had its trigger nailed down by
    a movement branch for exactly the same reason a fleeing one did. Same one
    line, same fix: it is in the `travel` list above, and a raider that parks on
    a shopping bot's doorstep now gets shot at while the shopping happens.

(b) The rest is tree PRIORITY, which is a different mechanism and is the design.
    `spend`, `haul` and `fix-base` sit ABOVE `potshot`/`attack`/`hunt` in all
    three trees, so a bot with a full hold or a purchase to make does that
    INSTEAD of picking a fight. Nothing is suppressed — the economy wins the
    tick. That is GDD §2.3's triangle and §2.9's "Easy attacks rarely", and
    inverting it would be a balance change nobody asked for.

(c) Banking cannot be suppressed by anything, for anyone.
    Since the v0.6 amendment the hold auto-drains inside your own station's
    atmosphere (`DEPOSIT_RANGE`, src/sim/step.ts auto-deposit) — no action, no
    verb, no wheel. A bot fighting inside its own atmosphere banks exactly like
    a human fighting there, because neither of them is doing anything.

(d) Mining and shooting are one trigger, so a human cannot do both either.
    Parity holds by construction (GDD §1: "your gun is your mining tool").

(e) The one real asymmetry left, reported and NOT fixed here.
    `defend` sits above `spend`, so a bot answering the alarm at its own docked
    station will not press the wheel while it fights — a human standing there
    could. It is left alone deliberately, because the GDD already ruled on the
    trade: §2.5, "Construction takes time — a turret assembles over ~10 seconds,
    a shield over ~15 — so defenses are bought before the attack, not during
    it", and §2.6, "a siege cannot be out-repaired" (which `wantsCorePatch`
    enforces directly as well). What a bot would buy mid-siege lands after the
    siege. Changing it is a design call for the Director, not a defect.


===============================================================================
6. SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS — what the balance crew should read
===============================================================================

Instrument: evidence/a0-81-fleeing-fire/contention-probe.ts, 48 full 2v2 team
matches (seeds 1..48, 300 s), both builds.

  build       ship deaths / match   ship damage / match   allied same-rock   enemy same-rock
  pre-a0-81           9.0                   564                4.06%              3.12%
  a0-81               5.0                   391                4.13%              1.89%

**Bot-vs-bot matches run 44% less lethal.** The mechanism is the developer's own
stated goal working: a bot that shoots back damages its pursuer, the pursuer
crosses its OWN nerve threshold and breaks off too, and a chase that used to end
in a kill now ends in two ships disengaging. Against a human this is the point —
a bot is meaningfully harder to farm, because a chase costs hull now. Bot
against bot it reads as mutual disengagement, and this is the number to watch if
that turns out to be too quiet.

That effect broke one existing test's control, and it is worth stating plainly:
src/bots/field-division.test.ts asserts allied rock contention as a RATIO
against an enemy-pair control, and friendly fire is off (GDD §1) — allies never
shoot each other — so a combat change can only ever move the denominator. The
allied number, which is the quantity that stage is about, did not move
(4.06% -> 4.13%). The bar was re-measured and an absolute assertion on the ally
rate was ADDED beside it, so the re-baseline leaves nothing unguarded. Full
reasoning in the test's own note.

Three seed-locked suites were re-baselined for a0-81, each with its measurement,
and none relaxed: ffa-parity's three golden hashes (a0-05 category — nothing in
a0-81 is team-aware, so it is not the leak Rule 3 forbids), team-winning's
fixture seed 13 -> 4 (fourth instance of a documented fixture fragility, seeds
1-16 re-scanned, largest margin picked), and field-division's ratio as above.


===============================================================================
7. WHAT PINS IT
===============================================================================

src/bots/behaviors.test.ts — `a retreating bot still fires on what is chasing
it`. Rusty, wounded, 900 units out from home, a hostile on the tail at 0.7 ×
weapon range on the far side from home (behind, not between — a hostile between
is a BLOCKADE and src/bots/cornered.ts owns that case). Asserts the stream
carries an aim action, that it bears on the chaser inside the tier's own spread
and not on the station, and that over a pinned three-second chase real
projectiles leave the barrel.

On the pre-fix build: `expected null not to be null` (no aim action at all) and
`rusty (easy) shoots back: expected 0 to be greater than 0`.

Six more cases beside it: the flight is untouched (thrust still carries the bot
home while the gun points the other way), every tier can, a teammate on the tail
draws nothing, empty space is still silent, a hauler gets the same gun, and
roam/scavenge stay deliberately unarmed.
