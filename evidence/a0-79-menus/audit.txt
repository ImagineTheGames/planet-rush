Read `./README.md` for how to re-run any number in here.


§0. WHAT WAS ASKED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Developer, 2026-08-17, with a screenshot of the main menu on a phone:

  "the buttons currently expand to fill the screen on mobile. can we
   reduce that so we have more empty space on the sides..."

  "I'd like to see a space background on every menu screen (the main
   title screen already has it, id like it to persist throughout)"

Two things. They are independent, they are both done, and each has its
own measurement below.


§1. THE MARGIN — WHY THE CAP NEVER BIT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
`src/ui/main-menu.ts` capped the plate column with ONE number:

    const columnWidth = Math.min(COLUMN.title, frame.band.width);

`Math.min` picks whichever is smaller. On a desktop that is the
ABSOLUTE (800) and the plates stay a column. On a phone it is the BAND —
752px at the developer's own 798x384 — so the cap never bit at all and
the plates took everything.

Measured on the SHIPPED bundle at that viewport: the plates were 752px
wide and the field beside them was **23px, or 2.88% of the screen on
each side**. That is the screenshot.

  before-menu.png    frames/798x384-landscape-phone/before-menu.png
  after-menu.png     frames/798x384-landscape-phone/after-menu.png


§1a. THE FIX, AND WHY THE PROPORTION IS NOT A TASTE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
`src/ui/gantry.ts` grows a proportional ceiling beside the absolute one
(`menuColumnWidth`, `menuColumnBand`). The share is NOT picked:

    MENU_COLUMN_SHARE = COLUMN.title / referenceBandWidth
                      = 800 / 1192 = 0.671

That is the ratified handoff's OWN ratio — it draws an 800px column in a
1192px band. Using the design's own number has two consequences, and
both of them are the point:

  1. the reference desktop is unchanged TO THE PIXEL (1192 x 0.671 =
     800 = COLUMN.title), so this is a ceiling and not a redesign. The
     same rule `frameMetrics` already keeps: a derivation must reproduce
     its own sample or it is a redesign;
  2. every narrower screen reads the SAME PROPORTION of field the
     handoff draws — 18.75% a side on a desktop, 18.4% on the phone.

A floor stops it descending where the constraint reverses
(`MENU_COLUMN_MIN = 448` reference px, scaled by `plateScale`): below
that width a screen gives up FIELD, never WORDS. The number is measured,
not chosen — it is the narrowest column the widest thing the front of
the game puts in one still fits at a phone's plate scale (the menu's
longest sub-line, 383px at 844x390; the settings screen's widest row is
345px). `main-menu.test.ts` re-measures it against the repo's own
advance tables, so copy that outgrows it fails loudly.

§1c. THE SAME RULE ON THE OTHER THREE SCREENS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
One seam, four screens, so the front of the game is one system rather
than four that drifted. Each screen names its own absolute column; the
proportion is decided in exactly one place.

  MAIN MENU  1 column.  800 absolute. Phone 752 -> 505.

  SETTINGS   1-2 columns (it wraps on a short phone). The ceiling is
             applied to the GRID, not to a column: `centeredGrid` splits
             the band between its columns, so with two columns the
             band's own half (372px) was already the narrower cap and
             capping a column alone changed nothing.
             On the 798x384 phone the FLOOR wins and the rows do not
             move — and that is the measured answer, not an omission: a
             column there is 372px holding content that measures 345px.
             27px of slack, and this does not spend them. At 844x390
             there is room: 2.84% -> 4.03% a side. Desktop unchanged.

  HANGAR     1 column, 1140 absolute (two panes want more room than one
             stack of plates). Phone 752 -> 505, desktop 1140 -> 800.
             Eyes on the frames: nothing is cut, the panes reflow, and
             the screen reads as a deliberate column rather than a
             stretched panel.

  CODEX      **2 content columns** — a rail AND an article. The floor is
             per column and this screen's article is a SCROLLING BODY OF
             TEXT: narrowing it does not reflow, it pushes paragraphs off
             the bottom. Measured on the shipped bundle at 798x384, a
             one-column floor took the article from "fits the band" to
             "cut mid-sentence", so the codex keeps its band on a phone.
             The ceiling does its work where the codex's real drift was:

               codex content    before    after
               798x384            752      752   (article stays whole)
               1280x720          1192      911
               3440x1440         3352     1140   <- see before-codex.png

             3352px of article on an ultrawide was one line of body text
             running three metres. That is fixed.


§2. THE SKY — THE REAL VOID, BEHIND EVERY MENU
----------------------------------------------------------------------
`VoidBackdrop` belonged to the MATCH renderer, so the game's own sky
disappeared the moment a player left the title gate and every menu was a
flat `PALETTE.vacuum` panel with nothing behind it.

`src/ui/menu-backdrop.ts` drives the REAL `VoidBackdrop` through its real
API — `setMap` / `configure` / `update` / `destroy`. **Not** a second star
field in CSS or in a local `Graphics`: one field, one set of numbers, one
thing to fix when the design moves. A second one would build the
instrument-vs-game disagreement that cost the bloom five rounds into the
product on purpose.

The sky is NAMED — Patina Drift, the hue the title gate's own wash is
painted in — and pinned to the map registry by a test. It had to be
named: the default map is `octagon` and `octagon`'s sky is `none`, so a
menu that took the default would have got no sky at all.

The five screens in the menu shell (title, settings, codex, hangar, and
the doors) stop painting their own full-screen ground when the shell says
the void is behind them (`setVoidBehind`). The DEFAULT is still "paint
it", because SettingsView has two homes: opened from the PAUSE overlay it
sits over a live match and its 0.96 scrim is doing real work.


§3. WHAT THE MENU BACKDROP COSTS PER FRAME — MEASURED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
a0-75 measured the void's cost while this brief was open: **2.33 blended
screenfuls per frame**, after its own sky-cache fix, and 61-95% of the
whole frame in a live match. That is the bill for a sky that MOVES.

A menu has no camera. So this takes the treatment the brief authorises
for a static screen — a STILL FRAME. `configure` and `update` run once
per RESIZE; the assembled void is then baked (`cacheAsTexture`, the same
mechanism `./screen-cache.ts` already uses on every menu screen) and
blitted. Per-frame cost is ONE OPAQUE TEXTURED QUAD.

`boundsArea` crops the bake to the SCREEN. Without it `cacheAsTexture`
sizes its texture from `getLocalBounds()`, and the parallax field is
~2.6x the viewport in each axis — a ~7x texture for no extra pixel on the
glass. `MENU_BAKE_MAX_TEXELS` (4 Mtexels = 16 MB) is a memory budget in
the same shape as a0-75's: a phone at dpr 3 bakes at full device
resolution, and only a very large or very dense viewport gives up
resolution (~0.9 of CSS on an ultrawide at dpr 2) rather than 79 MB.

THE MEASUREMENT (frame-cost.txt). One bundle, `?sky=0` vs `?sky=1`, three
alternated passes taken back to back, ~3 s window each, with the live
match sampled in the same run as the denominator:

  viewport            device px    sky=0     sky=1    backdrop    match   menu/match
  phone 798x384        1.23 Mpx    22.5 ms   36.0 ms   +13.5 ms   135 ms      0.27x
  desktop 1280x720     0.92 Mpx    16.1 ms   26.6 ms   +10.5 ms   167 ms      0.16x
  ultrawide 3440x1440  4.95 Mpx    85.7 ms  151.1 ms   +65.4 ms   653 ms      0.23x

READ IT LIKE THIS. **This box has no GPU** (SwiftShader), so not one of
those milliseconds is the developer's. Two things in the table do travel:

  1. the cost is LINEAR IN PIXELS at 11.0 / 11.4 / 13.2 ms per megapixel
     across a 5.4x range of viewport. That is the signature of exactly
     ONE screenful of fill — which is the claim, arrived at from the
     other end;
  2. `menu/match` is **0.16x-0.27x**, against the 4x ceiling
     `tests/mobile/menu-frame-cost.spec.ts` gates on. The static front
     door costs a quarter of what the running game costs, at every
     viewport measured, with the sky on.

The +1 screenful is inherent to the request and is not avoidable: showing
a background behind a translucent foreground costs a second screenful. A
LIVE void would have cost 2.33 of them and would have scaled with a0-75's
per-pixel bill on every frame; this pays it once per resize.


§4. WHAT IS NOT IN THIS BRANCH
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- The HUD. a0-74 is binding it to a content box on ultrawides; nothing
  here touches `hud-geometry.ts` or the layout registry.
- The LOBBY, ship select, map select and end-of-match. They live in a
  different shell (`openLobby`) and take the same
  `Math.min(COLUMN.title, ...)` shape at `end-of-match.ts:722` and
  `lobby-geometry.ts:2010`. They are the obvious next surface; the brief
  named four screens and widening the diff into a0-74's neighbourhood
  during the same week is how two lanes collide.
- The TITLE GATE's own canvas star field. It is a DOM overlay that seals
  over everything, and the developer's ask was for the sky to persist
  PAST it, which it now does.

Found and fixed on the way past, in one line, because the teardown list
was right there: `hangarView` was added to the shell root at a0-14 and
never removed or destroyed with the rest.


§6. A THING THE GOLDEN GATE CANNOT SEE — found on the way past
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The re-baseline moved eleven images. Seven of them moved on the FIRST
`--update-snapshots` run; four did not, and passed against their old,
sky-less baselines:

    desktop-title, desktop-settings, desktop-doors, phone-landscape-doors

Those four are exactly the menu screens whose GEOMETRY did not change —
only the sky went in behind them. The seven that moved all had a plate
column or a content band move as well.

It is not that the sky is missing on them. `./sky-check.mjs` shoots each
screen twice from the shipped bundle, `?sky=1` against `?sky=0`, and
counts the pixels that differ:

    desktop 1280x800    menu    21.00% differ
    desktop 1280x800    doors   20.93% differ
    phone 844x390 dpr3  menu    15.24% differ
    phone 844x390 dpr3  doors   17.29% differ

Every one of those is far past the goldens' own `maxDiffPixelRatio` of
1%. And deleting `desktop-title-desktop-linux.png` and re-running that
one test wrote a 395 KB starry frame where the old baseline was 39 KB and
flat — the capture was right all along.

So the comparison is what did not fire. The likely reason is Playwright's
per-pixel `threshold` (default 0.2 of a normalised YIQ distance): this
void is Floor `#070910` where the panel was Vacuum `#0d1015`, plus a
low-alpha patina wash and a scatter of small stars. Nearly every pixel it
changes, it changes by a little, and "a little" on a near-black frame is
under the per-pixel threshold — so fewer than 1% of pixels are counted as
different at all.

**That is a blind spot in the gate, not in this branch**, and it is worth
knowing: a change that alters the whole frame *gently* is invisible to
these baselines. It is QA's file and QA's number, so nothing here touches
it — but the four stale baselines were deleted and regenerated by hand
(a MISSING snapshot is always written, which is the one path that cannot
be silently skipped), so all eleven committed images depict what ships.
