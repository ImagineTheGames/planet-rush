Planet Rush · Concept Board · Facility Directions (M0 pattern, like maps r2)

The home is now a mining facility.
Pick the direction before we rebuild the biggest sprite.

GDD v0.7 lifts the fiction from "planets" to deliberately-sited mining facilities on a contested claim — a fiction-only pivot, no mechanic moved (§0). §5.4 keeps the round claimed-planetoid as the body and flags one open Art call: does the facility gain industrial dressing — rigs, gantries, docking arms — beyond that body? This board answers with three buildable directions. Each keeps the planetoid at its heart (so every readability contract survives) and dresses it differently. You pick one on the board; the build brief follows the pick. Nothing here changes shipped game art — it is a decision aid.

The contract every direction keepsWhat must still read at a glance

These are not negotiable per direction — they are the readability contracts (style-guide §9, GDD §2.2/§5.4). Whatever dressing wins, the reactor is still the yellow win-condition, the rings are still owner-colour + red-fill, the halo still lands on DEPOSIT_RANGE, and the derelict still goes cold. A direction is judged on whether its silhouette hosts all of this naturally.

The radial stack

Every gauge on a facility is concentric. A direction must sit inside this envelope.

Collection field · DEPOSIT_RANGE

Owner-only air-glow to exactly 4× the body radius (256u). This is where you bank.

Ring grammar & nameplate stack

Owner ring = HP remaining; red fills clockwise from 12 o'clock = HP lost. Shields before reactor.

The turret pool (the "§5.5 pool") — 4 mounts per facility, one escalating story

Mk I→III ride the upgrade ladder as more-gun silhouettes; the barrel-less Dome is the reserved enemy-field / derelict variant. Every direction must seat these naturally on its 4 mount points.

Direction AThe Ring-Rig — orbital refinery ring around the claimed core

A rotating refinery gantry encircles the planetoid; the reactor glows at the rock's heart, silos and docking arms hang off the ring. The most circular of the three — it inherits the radial stack almost for free, and the gantry nodes are natural turret mounts. Reads instantly as "a station built onto a rock." The safe, production-first pick.

At rest

Beacon + nameplate + 4 seated turrets + reactor + collection-field lap.

Under siege

Damage ring fills red (40% lost) · shield failing & holed · turrets firing · red edge-pulse.

Reactor & shield fit

Yellow win-condition core inside the ring; shield clears the gantry cleanly.

Derelict — "abandoned rig"

Ash body, reactor out, gantry snapped, ore-laden debris. No red, no owner colour.

Layout-registry implications
  • Cleanest fit of the three. Body stays a true disc of radius R, so beacon / damage / shield / halo rings and the turret orbit rim need zero registry change — the dressing lives entirely inside R.
  • Gantry must sit inboard of the turret orbit rim (≈1.2R). Turrets glide around the rim (sim orbitSpeed); keep the outermost gantry element ≤ R so a gliding Mk III never clips a docking arm. Silos/arms drawn at ≤0.97R here.
  • Mount points = 4 gantry nodes at the diagonals, clear of the top (nameplate) — matches capPerPlanet:4. No new hit-test geometry; turrets still mount on the rim.
  • Four planetoid variants become four gantry-rotation seeds (spoke phase + arm count) — variety from arrangement, no new hue, same determinism discipline as the current planet variants.

Read & tone: arcade-legible and busy in a good way — clearly a working rig. Risk: the ring can read as "just a station" and lose the planetoid; mitigated by keeping the reactor + patina continents visible through the gantry. Cheapest to build and the lowest-risk on every contract.

Direction BThe Refinery Platform — an angular deck bolted onto the planetoid

A hard-edged hexagonal refinery deck is clamped over the sunward face of the rock: tanks, a cracking stack, catwalks, and a reactor well sunk into the deck centre. Semi-angular — it breaks the pure circle for a stronger industrial read, while the dark planetoid still shows at the limb so the rings keep a round envelope. The middle option: more character than A, less risk than C.

At rest

Beacon + nameplate + 4 corner-bastion turrets + reactor well + halo lap.

Under siege

Damage ring 40% · deck shield holed · stack & tanks take fire · red edge-pulse.

Reactor & shield fit

Reactor well glows up out of a dark rim; shield rounds off the hex corners.

Derelict — "abandoned rig"

Cold deck, dead well, scorched tanks, ore debris. Nothing burns.

Layout-registry implications
  • Deck must inscribe within radius R (hex circum-radius ≤ R = 0.83R across the flats). The rings hug the planetoid limb, not the deck edge — so the round envelope is untouched and the beacon/damage/halo generators are unchanged.
  • Turret orbit rim stays circular and clear. Turrets mount on the four corner bastions but glide on the round rim (as today), so a bastion is a visual seat, not a hit-test change; keep bastions ≤ R so a gliding turret never overlaps the deck.
  • Deck orientation is per-facility fixed (flat edge to sunward). A rotating deck would fight the gliding turrets; lock it, and let turrets slide over it — one registry note for the renderer, no sim change.
  • Four variants = four deck layouts (tank/stack arrangement) over the same hex — arrangement variety, palette-clean.

Read & tone: the strongest "industrial refinery" read while staying arcade-bright; the sunk reactor well is a distinctive focal. Risk: the flat-to-round transition at the limb needs care so the deck doesn't look like a sticker — solved with the dark planetoid crescent showing past the deck. Moderate build cost.

Direction CThe Extractor Bastion — gothic-brutalist fortress over a deep reactor shaft

Heavy faceted armour cladding with four buttress spurs (a top-down star-fort) and a deep central extraction shaft stepping down to the reactor glowing at the bottom. The most serious silhouette — it leans hardest into "homes are the one serious thing" (§8), and the spurs make the most menacing turret bastions. The highest drama, and the highest registry risk.

At rest

Beacon + nameplate + 4 spur-mounted turrets + shaft reactor + halo lap.

Under siege

Damage ring 40% · shield holed · spur guns firing · red edge-pulse. The fortress bleeds.

Reactor & shield fit

The shaft: dark rings stepping down to a hot core; shield caps the fortress.

Derelict — "abandoned rig"

Gutted fortress, shaft gone dark, spurs broken, ore debris. Coldest of the three.

Layout-registry implications
  • Highest registry risk of the three. The spurs are the only elements that want to push past R. They must be clamped inside R (spur tips ≤ 0.97R here) or the beacon / damage / halo rings float off a non-circular mass and stop reading as clean gauges. This is the one direction where "keep it inside the disc" needs active discipline.
  • Turret orbit rim vs. fixed bastions. The fantasy is guns on the spurs, but the sim glides turrets around the rim. Two honest options for the brief: (a) keep gliding and let a turret slide across spur tops (spurs ≤ R, decorative), or (b) propose a sim amendment to pin turrets to 4 fixed bastion slots (a real rule change — separate, flagged commit, not unilateral). A picks the pick.
  • Deep reactor shaft reads as depth, not a flat disc — strongest reactor focal, but the dark shaft rim eats contrast; keep the hot core ≥ the current core radius so it still reads at 24px and minimap scale.
  • Four variants = four cladding facet + rib seeds. Nameplate must clear the tallest spur (top mount kept off 12 o'clock).

Read & tone: the most memorable and the most on-tone for the "serious home" beat — a burning fortress and a gutted one both land hard. Risk: it is the least arcade-cheeky, the most expensive to build, and the only one that may want a sim conversation about fixed mounts. Highest ceiling, highest cost.
