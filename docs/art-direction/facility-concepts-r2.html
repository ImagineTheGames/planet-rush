Art · facility body · round 2 · l1-02b

They have to look like mining stations

Round 1 was denied in full, and the reason was one sentence. This board is the answer to that sentence and nothing else: three directions that are three different objects, each of which you can watch doing the job before you read a label. Nothing about the palette, the rings or the mechanics has moved.

Amendment · 2026-08-07 · the hull carries no turrets. The developer, on the new mining station: “an ammendment to the new mining station, we dont need the turrets on it, those will be built externally as it already is….” Round 2 shipped with four turrets drawn into every facility body and the DEFEND card called that part “unchanged from round 1 — this part was never the problem.” It was the problem, and not on taste: a turret is a bought, capped, individually destructible building (GDD §2.5, §2.6), so a hull that draws four of its own would show eight on a station with four built, would make a fresh spawn read as a fortress with none built, and would leave a shot-down turret still standing on the hull — killing the re-arm tell exactly where the player looks for it. So the guns come off all three directions, D, E and F, and the mounting ground stays. Every lug, pad and keyway drawn here is a seat: it is where a turret the owner buys will stand, and it is empty until they buy one. Nothing else on this board moved — no ring, no radius, no constant, and no part of the mining read the directions were drawn for. Enforced in code as a named exclusion (STATION_HULL_EXCLUSIONS, src/art/stations.ts), asserted from both sides in src/art/compliance.test.ts: the hull emits nothing on the list, and the bought-turret path is untouched.

The verdictWhat you said

“none of these look like a mining space station”

DENY ALL · facility-concepts.html · 2026-08-06T01:31:49Z

Taken literally: a viewer should identify the thing as a place where ore is extracted and processed in space before reading any label. The names already said mining. The pictures did not. Here is what they were actually saying instead.

  • All three were the same object. A round planetoid with machinery arranged around it — a ring, a plate, a set of spurs. The body carried the silhouette and the machinery was dressing laid over a world.
  • Nothing was visibly extracting anything. No cut face, no teeth, no hole, no working wall. A rig that is not opening ground is a building.
  • There was no ore you could see. No bin with a level in it, no stockpile, no spill — nothing that says the thing is full of the stuff it exists to get.
  • Nothing moved. No chute, no conveyor, no barge: no path from where ore comes out to where it goes.
  • The outlines were radially symmetric. That is what planets are, and what working plants never are.

The standing checkSix things you must be able to see

The test each direction is built against, and the shared industrial vocabulary all three are assembled from. Every yellow mark on this board is ore, molten ore, an ore level, hazard tape, or the core — the five things style-guide §2 allows signal yellow to be. Nothing here is decoration.

EXTRACT

A cut face you can see: teeth, a kerf, ore in the rock they are biting. If nothing is being opened, nothing is being mined.

STORE

Vessels whose CONTENTS you can read — a level ring on a silo, a level bar in a hopper. Ore visible in storage is the cheapest mining cue there is.

HANDLE

Trusses, chutes and conveyors with ore riding them, and spill where it doesn't quite make it. Ore has to travel from where it comes out to where it goes.

PROCESS

A smelter with molten ore in the slot, a stack with a dust plume, and a radiator comb. Heat rejection is the tell that says industrial plant in vacuum.

DEFEND

The owner ring, threat red filling it clockwise as the core is lost, and clear, unoccupied mounting ground on the body radius. The hull carries no gun of its own: turrets are bought at the Build & Upgrade wheel and stand as their own sprites, so a station with none built has to look like it has none. (Amended 2026-08-07 — see the amendment note above.)

WEAR

Rivets where plates meet, a lit top edge and a shadowed under-line (Gantry/Bone), hazard tape at the drops, and patina creeping out of every mouth.

Direction DThe Cutterhead — the machine that digs

A rotary bore head clamped onto the claim by eight anchor lugs, seen down its own throat. The cutter ring's sixteen teeth bite inward at a rock face with ore seams running through it; the fresh kerf is the bright arc where it is cutting right now. What it wins is lifted out on a chute into an ore circuit that rings the deck — two hoppers with their levels showing, a smelter, a radiator comb hung outboard, and an apron where the barge takes the product away. The spoil boom throws the waste clear.

D · live, at rest — every callout is a job it is doing

Owner P1 Azure. Body radius = STATION.radius (64 world units); the core, damage ring and beacon ring sit exactly where the shipped sprite puts them.

Why it reads as a mining station: The object is a machine, not a body. Its outline is lugged and toothed, its middle is a hole with a working face in it, and one long arm leaves the circle entirely. Nothing here is a sphere, and the first thing you find in the centre is rock being cut with ore in it.

The hard partDoes it survive at the size the game draws it?

Two tests, and one correction. The camera renders 1:1, so a facility is exactly 128 px across — on a 390 px phone that is a third of the screen width, which is the size these designs are really judged at.

The minimap, checked rather than assumed: src/ui/minimap.ts draws a station as an owner-coloured dot (STATION_DOT_FRACTION = 0.04 of the map square, floor 2 px), not as a sprite — so there is no silhouette on the minimap to fail. The honest small-scale test is the downscale strip below: the shape has to stay itself as it shrinks toward the far-zoom read, and none of the three may collapse into the same grey disc round 1 collapsed into.

UnchangedThe palette this board is drawn in

The six frozen colours, plus the declared value-ramp shades from src/art/palette.ts and one owner roster hue. No seventh hue enters on a concept board. Signal yellow appears on this board only as: ore in a hopper or a stockpile, an ore-level ring, molten ore in a smelter or crucible, hazard tape at a drop or a loading edge, ore seams in rock, ore riding a conveyor, and the core. Threat red appears only on the damage ring, enemy fire and the siege alarm — and never on a derelict.
