Planet Rush — Concept Boards

Day-0 concept mode, per GDD §4.7. Ten visual directions, each rendering the same scene: your ship mining under pressure while your planet sits defended, an enemy inbound. Judged against the tone paragraph: "Saturday-morning space brawl: fast, bright, a little cheeky — arcade on the surface, a small ache underneath."

Theme A

Neon Vector

Scene — mining while the alarm is quiet

Prototype sprites — ship liveries · turret · shield · asteroid · ore · wreck

Palette

Space #05060C

Player/ally #40F0D0

Threat #FF5EA0

Ore/economy #FFD25E

Shields/UI #7BD4FF

Neutral rock #B8C2E0

Read on tone: fast and bright, yes — cheeky, less so; neon reads "arcade-intense" more than "Saturday-morning." Cost: cheapest to generate procedurally (strokes + glow filter), instancing-friendly, best legibility of the three at small sizes. Risk: most generic — reads as "another Geometry Wars-like."

Theme B

Space #1B2B4D

Player red #F25C54

Planet green #4FC978

Ore gold #FFD84D

Shield blue #9ADCFF

Read on tone: the closest match — toy ships, fireworks explosions, bots that look like a cartoon cast; the "small ache" lands because the cheerful wreck stays on the map. Cost: medium — thick-outline shapes are very achievable procedurally (SVG paths + flat fills), liveries are just palette swaps. Risk: chunky outlines need care at small zoom; readability solved by silhouette, not detail.

Read on tone: the most *personality* — finned rockets and ray-gun turrets are inherently cheeky, and the muted palette gives the planet-death beat its ache for free. Cost: highest of the three — duotone shading, halftone patterns, and serif UI need more generation care and a custom font fallback plan. Risk: lowest contrast; ore and threats pop less at speed than in A or B.

Theme F

Paper Cutout

Felt night #232B3A

Player coral #F4715F

Planet #5BBF7A

Ore #FFD269

Shield #9AD7F5

Paper #F2E9D8

Read on tone: warmest and most disarming of the ten; a paper planet tearing in half is a genuinely affecting death moment. Cost: medium — torn-edge polygons and drop shadows are procedural-friendly, but "handmade wobble" needs a jitter pass to avoid looking accidentally sloppy. Risk: drop shadows on hundreds of entities need cheap faking (baked offsets, not filters) to hold 60 fps.

Read on tone: calm, modern, friendly — more "beautiful mobile game" than "brawl"; cheek would have to come from animation and bot barks, not the art. Theme I

Hull grey #8C8577

Rust #7A4A2B

Hazard amber #FFB02E

Torch #FF5A1F

Read on tone: the deliberate counter-option — grubby, funny in a junkyard way, and the mining fantasy (cutting torches, hazard stripes, ore veins) is strongest here. Furthest from "Saturday-morning." Cost: medium-high — texture detail (rivets, rust bands) multiplies across every sprite. Risk: mud — brown-on-brown at speed; needs the amber/torch colors to carry all gameplay signals.
