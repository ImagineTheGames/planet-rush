# a0-130 — the Easy pool never kills anything, so its winner is whoever was shot at least, and that is Bolt

## 2 · The mechanism, before anything is tuned

The brief's first instruction is to establish *why* before turning anything, and
it names three candidates: the other Easy seats being too timid, low caution
simply being strong at this skill, or a0-105/a0-107's standoff work suiting Bolt.

None of them is where the answer starts. The answer starts with a fact about the
pool that no report in this series has stated, because no report in this series
had opened a match up to look:

### 2.1 · Nothing in an Easy match ever destroys a reactor in play

`./autopsy.ts` re-runs Easy-pool matches with the world open instead of the
result sealed, and records what `runBotMatch` does not: the core HP of every seat
at the tick the collapse opened, who had landed damage on whom (off the sim's own
`combat-credit` ledger, not inferred), and what each home had standing.

```
=== Easy-pool autopsy: 256 matches (128 seeds x 2 rotations) ===
decided 43  drawn 213 (83.2%)

--- 1. does anything reach a core before the collapse? ---
  rusty : 1024 seats, core never touched in 218 (21.3%), mean core at collapse 96.1%, mean floor before collapse 96.1%
  bolt  : 1024 seats, core never touched in 419 (40.9%), mean core at collapse 97.9%, mean floor before collapse 98.0%

--- 1b. who does the chipping? ---
  rusty : dealt 24.5 HP/match to enemy stations; its own home was last hit by { bolt: 90.2%, rusty: 9.8% }
  bolt  : dealt 379.4 HP/match to enemy stations; its own home was last hit by { bolt: 43.0%, rusty: 57.0% }

--- 1c. what did each character buy? (standing at collapse) ---
  rusty : mean 2.96 turrets, 1.00 shields standing when the field closed
  bolt  : mean 3.00 turrets, 0.90 shields standing when the field closed

--- 2. the margin: how far apart are the top two cores at collapse? ---
  decided: median 1.7% of a core  max 3.5%
  drawn  : median 0.0% of a core  max 0.0%

--- 3. does the winner own the highest core at collapse? ---
  winner held the (uniquely) highest core at collapse in 43/43 (100.0%), shared the top in a further 0

--- 4. the pool, one row per character ---
  rusty : 11/43 decided (25.6%)
  bolt  : 32/43 decided (74.4%)
```

*Hand-written reading.* **Every Easy match reaches the collapse with all eight
homes alive.** Not most — all of them, on every seed the autopsy ran. Neither
Easy character has a branch that attacks a station: `easy.ts`'s own module note
says so in as many words ("there is no seek-and-destroy branch at all"), and the
one leaf that fires at anything, `potshot`, engages *ships*.

So the reactors that die in an Easy match are killed by the **collapse**, which
decays every core at `COLLAPSE_CORE_DECAY` — 1 HP/s, against `CORE_HP` 100 — and
which therefore kills them in the order they were already standing in.

### 2.2 · The winner is a bookkeeping comparison, decided by about one and a half HP

If entropy is the only thing killing cores and it eats every core at one rate,
then the last core standing is simply the **highest core when the field closed**.
The autopsy above confirms it without exception, and
`tests/harness/a0-130-easy-pool.test.ts` pins it so the reading cannot quietly
stop being true.

Two things follow, and both of them reframe the target:

- **A draw is a tie in that number.** If the top core is shared, every remaining
  home dies on one tick, nobody outlived anybody, and a0-113 rules it a draw. The
  Easy pool's famous 79.6% draw rate is not eight bots failing to find
  each other. It is eight *undamaged* reactors arriving at the collapse on the
  same HP and going out together.
- **A win is the opposite of a tie, and nothing more.** In the decided matches
  the gap between the highest reactor and the second highest is a median of
  1.7% of a core. On a 100 HP core that is about one and a half hit points.

**The 55% ceiling is being read off a quantity with a signal of one and a half
HP.** That is worth saying plainly before any of the arms below, because it is
the reason every intuition about this pool is wrong: nothing in it is a fight
being won.

### 2.3 · There is exactly one gun in the pool

If the result is "whose reactor was shot least", the next question is who is
doing the shooting. `easy.ts` gates its only offensive leaf on the character:

```
when('potshot', (ctx) => ctx.weights.triangle.attack >= EASY_ATTACK_WEIGHT && …)
```

`EASY_ATTACK_WEIGHT` is 0.4. Bolt's attack weight is 0.65 and Rusty's is 0.1, so
**Bolt is the only Easy character that can fire at another ship at all** — and
41.7% of Bolt's decisions in this pool are that leaf, against Rusty's 0.0%:

| character | `potshot` | `hunt` | `mine` | `haul` | `spend` | `defend` | `retreat` | `turn-and-fight` | `cornered-fight` | `fix-base` | `scavenge` | `roam` | `dead` | deaths / seat-match |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **rusty** | 0.0% | 0.0% | 29.9% | 6.9% | 17.2% | 11.8% | 20.3% | 3.4% | 2.0% | 0.7% | 0.8% | 0.7% | 6.5% | 11.8 |
| **bolt** | 41.7% | 0.0% | 6.1% | 4.0% | 0.3% | 13.9% | 10.3% | 4.0% | 0.5% | 0.4% | 0.2% | 0.2% | 18.7% | 32.5 |

The arm that tests it is `d-bolt-attack`, which moves Bolt's attack weight to
0.35 — below the gate, and nothing else:

*Hand-written reading.* **Zero decided matches out of 512.** Take the one gun out
of the Easy pool and it stops producing winners entirely: every match ties, every
match draws, and the contest the 55% ceiling is applied to does not exist. That
is the cleanest available statement of what the Easy-pool win rate measures.
It measures **where Bolt's misses landed**.

### 2.4 · And the retreat is what delivers them

Bolt shoots at ships, not at homes, so a reactor is only chipped when a firefight
happens *on top of one*. Which home? The autopsy answers it: Rusty's reactors are
last hit by Bolt in the overwhelming majority of seats, and Bolt deals better than
ten times the station damage Rusty does.

The delivery mechanism is the **retreat**, and it is a design rule doing exactly
what it was written to do. `behaviors.ts` `retreat` runs a frightened bot *home*,
into its own turret cover (GDD §2.6) — unless the threat is already there. The
retreat threshold is the tier's `retreatHullFraction` (0.5 at Easy, the highest in
the game) scaled by the character's `caution`:

| character | `caution` | breaks off at | share of its decisions spent in `retreat` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rusty | 1.3 | **65%** of hull | 20.3% |
| Bolt | 0.5 | **25%** of hull | 10.3% |

So Rusty spends a fifth of its match running home with the pool's only gun behind
it, and the misses land on the reactor it ran to. Bolt, breaking off at a quarter
hull, does that half as often — and nothing chases it home anyway, because Rusty
cannot shoot.

**Bolt does not win the Easy pool by attacking. It wins by not running away.**

Three arms price that chain, all on the same seeds. The tier's retreat threshold
is the master dial of the whole contest:

| `retreatHullFraction` (Easy) | Rusty breaks off at | Bolt breaks off at | decided of 512 | top of the pool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.2** (Hard's) | 26% | 10% | **17 (3.3%)** | `rusty` 52.9% |
| **0.5** (shipped) | 65% | 25% | 95 (18.6%) | `bolt` 67.4% |
| **0.8** | 100% — always | 40% | **191 (37.3%)** | `bolt` 77.0% |

*Hand-written reading.* **Make the tier braver and the contest evaporates; make
it more timid and Bolt's share grows.** The two are the same phenomenon seen from
two ends: retreats are what put reactors in front of the gun, so the amount of
retreating in the pool sets both how often the pool has a winner *and* how far
ahead the character that retreats least finishes. This is the finding the rest of
the report is downstream of, and it is why no arm below manages to raise the
decide rate without also raising Bolt.

### 2.5 · The tier that never contests the endgame

One more structural fact, because it explains the draw rate that makes this pool
so hard to measure. `medium.ts` and `hard.ts` both carry

```
when('hunt', (ctx) => ctx.view.collapsed, (ctx) => hunt(ctx, nearestLivingRival(ctx))),
```

and `behaviors.ts` `hunt` says in its own doc that this is *"the bots keeping
their half"* of the ruleset's promise that a match cannot stalemate (GDD §2.3).
`easy.ts` has no such branch. The three pools line up with that exactly:

| pool | contestants | matches | decided | draw rate | has a collapse branch? | top of the pool | exact 95% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `easy` | 2 | 512 | 95 | **81.4%** | **no** | `bolt` 67.4% | 57.0% – 76.6% |
| `medium` | 2 | 512 | 425 | **17.0%** | yes — `hunt` | `patch` 57.6% | 52.8% – 62.4% |
| `hard` | 3 | 768 | 766 | **0.3%** | yes — `hunt` | `warden` 40.3% | 36.8% – 43.9% |

*Hand-written reading.* The tier with no endgame draws four matches in five; the
tier with one draws three in a thousand. Note the Medium row on the way past: it
is the other two-horse pool, and its top sits at 57.6% on an interval that
contains the ceiling — **UNRESOLVED**, not a finding, and not this brief's
target, but the same shape of problem §5.2 comes back to. The last **100 seconds** of every Easy
match — the whole collapse, an eighth of the match — is eight bots mining a field
that has stopped producing while entropy picks a winner by a hit point and a
half. §6.1 builds the missing branch, measures it, and does not ship it.

### 3.1 · "The other Easy seats are too timid" — measured, and **false**

`d-rusty-attack` gives Rusty Bolt's own attack weight, so both seats can fire.

*Hand-written reading.* It transforms the *pool* and leaves *Bolt* exactly where
it was. The decide rate goes from 18.6% to 60.5%: 310 decided matches
instead of 95, more than three times the evidence — and Bolt takes **69.4%** of
them, exact 95% 63.9% – 74.4%, which is not distinguishable from the 67.4% it
started at and is **further above the ceiling than before**, because the interval
is now three times narrower.

This is the most important negative result in the report, because arming the
other seat is the obvious reading of "the pool needs raising" and it is the one
the brief explicitly licenses. It does not work. A second gun does not make Rusty
harder to chase home; it just adds a second source of stray fire to a map where
the fights already happen at Rusty's front door.

### 3.2 · "a0-105/a0-107's standoff work suits Bolt" — measured, and **no**

Bolt has the shortest patience in the cast — 1.5 s of a failing retreat before it
turns and fights — and the brief names that as a candidate. `d-easy-patience`
takes the whole tier's `standoffPatienceSeconds` from 3 to Hard's 1.2, which
compresses the spread between the two characters to 1.56 s and 0.6 s.

*Hand-written reading.* Bolt goes from 67.4% to **65.0%**, and the two intervals
overlap along almost their whole length. a0-105 and a0-107's dial is not what is
holding this number up. That is worth filing on its own: the standoff work is the
most recently changed thing in this tree and it is the first thing a balance
brief would reach for, and it is measurably not the cause.

### 3.3 · "Low caution is simply strong at this skill" — measured, and **yes**

It is the only dial on Bolt that moves the number at all, and it moves it a long
way. Bolt's `caution` at 0.9 gives 62.9%; at Rusty's 1.3 the pool *turns over* and
Rusty tops it at 57.1%.

*Hand-written reading.* §2.4 is why. `caution` is the retreat multiplier, the
retreat runs the bot home, and running home is what feeds a reactor to the pool's
only gun. A Bolt with Rusty's nerve retreats like Rusty, is chased home like
Rusty, and loses like Rusty.

**And look at what that arm actually achieved.** At `caution` 1.3 the *top of the
pool* is Rusty, at a point estimate above the ceiling and on an UNRESOLVED
interval. The nerf did not demonstrate the target met; it moved the crown. §5.2
runs the same arm four times deeper and the picture gets sharper, not kinder.

## 4 · The one thing that never mattered

`d-easy-norepair` sets `EASY_REPAIR_AT` to zero, so no Easy bot ever patches its
reactor. The repair ration is the obvious suspect in a contest decided by core
HP, and `behaviors.ts` `repairTargetFraction` is scaled by `caution` — the same
dial §3.3 just found — so it looked like the mechanism.

*Hand-written reading.* The arm came back **bit-identical to the shipped run**:
95 decided of 512, 64 of them Bolt's, the same 67.4%. Not close — identical, row
for row. Easy bots in this pool never repair at all, because the chip damage is
so light that no core ever drops under the gate. A dial that looked central turns
out never to fire, and the only reason anybody knows that is that the arm was run
instead of reasoned about.

**On the clustering table, honestly.** a0-126 ran this check because its cast
contest played 32 map draws seven times each, and seven matches from one map lean
together. This pool cannot have that problem for a reason worth stating rather
than hiding behind a design effect of 1.000: it draws four matches in five, so
1675 decided matches are spread over 1481 seeds — about
one apiece. There is essentially nothing left inside a cluster to correlate, the
raw ICC is meaninglessly negative, and the estimator floors it at zero. The plain
binomial reading is the fair one here, but it is fair because the draws already
scattered the sample, not because the check found anything.

### 5.2 · The forbidden nerf, run deep anyway

The brief forbids tuning Bolt into a cautious bot to hit a number, and §3.3 has
already shown that `caution` is the only dial that moves it. That is an argument
for not shipping the nerf; it is not an argument for not *measuring* it, because
"the nerf we were told not to make would not have worked either" is a much
stronger thing to file than "we were told not to".

| contestant | screen · 256 seeds | deep · 1024 seeds | move | exact 95% (deep) | width before | width after | verdict (deep) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bolt | 42 / 98 (**42.9%**) | 179 / 387 (**46.3%**) | +3.4 pts | 41.2% – 51.4% | 20.3 pts | 10.2 pts | INSIDE |
| rusty | 56 / 98 (**57.1%**) | 208 / 387 (**53.7%**) | -3.4 pts | 48.6% – 58.8% | 20.3 pts | 10.2 pts | **UNRESOLVED** |

*Hand-written reading.* At Rusty's `caution` the pool **turns over**: the top of
it is `rusty` at 53.7%, exact 95% confidence interval
48.6% – 58.8% — **UNRESOLVED**, because the interval still contains the
ceiling.

Read that carefully, because it is not the result a lane hoping to ship a nerf
would want and it is not the result one hoping to avoid shipping one would want
either. The strongest available nerf to Bolt — the one that costs the character
everything it is — takes the pool from a resolved **72.1% OVER** to an
**UNRESOLVED** 53.7% *with a different character on top*. It did not
demonstrate the target met. Settling that would take **6668 decided matches** — 17.2× this arm, and at its 81.1% draw rate that is **35287 matches to run**, against
the 387 this arm has.

So the honest statement is: the forbidden nerf buys an unproven maybe, on the
other seat, at the cost of the character the brief exists to protect.

That is not an accident of these two characters. **The Easy pool is a two-horse
contest**, so the two rates sum to 100% and a 55% ceiling on the top of it
demands the pool sit inside 45–55% — a ten-point window. The Hard pool has three
contestants and the same ceiling gives it twenty-two points of room; the
ship-class contest has four and gets thirty. The Easy and Medium pools are held
to a standard three times tighter than everything else the ceiling is applied to,
and nothing in GDD §3.8 says that was intended. §11 takes that to the Director.

## 6 · The levers, priced

### 6.1 · The endgame — built, measured, and reverted

§2.5 found a real gap: Easy is the only tier with no collapse branch, and it
draws four matches in five. Commit `7ca2d7f1` closes it. `endgameSiege` fires the
gun where `behaviors.ts` `hunt` only closes, because this tier has no `attack`
branch beneath the leaf to pull the trigger; it sits below `last-stand`,
`cornered-fight`, `retreat`, `defend` and `defend-ally`, so the cast survives the
phase change with no new dial — a timid character spends the collapse answering
its own alarm and a reckless one spends it on somebody's doorstep.

| contestant | screen · 256 seeds | deep · 1024 seeds | move | exact 95% (deep) | width before | width after | verdict (deep) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bolt | 212 / 225 (**94.2%**) | 833 / 875 (**95.2%**) | +1.0 pts | 93.6% – 96.5% | 6.6 pts | 3.0 pts | **OVER** |
| rusty | 13 / 225 (**5.8%**) | 42 / 875 (**4.8%**) | -1.0 pts | 3.5% – 6.4% | 6.6 pts | 3.0 pts | INSIDE |

*Hand-written reading.* **It fixes the absence and hands Bolt the match.** The
draw rate falls from 79.6% to 57.3% — the pool finally has a
contest — and Bolt takes **95.2%** of it, exact 95% confidence
interval 93.6% – 96.5%. Not 72.1%: 95.2%. Nineteen of every
twenty decided matches.

The reason is §2.4 again, one phase later, and the leaf census of the arm itself
says it out loud:

| character | `potshot` | `hunt` | `mine` | `haul` | `spend` | `defend` | `retreat` | `turn-and-fight` | `cornered-fight` | `fix-base` | `scavenge` | `roam` | `dead` | deaths / seat-match |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **rusty** | 0.0% | 1.2% | 26.9% | 6.2% | 16.1% | 15.3% | 20.3% | 3.4% | 2.0% | 0.7% | 0.6% | 0.4% | 7.0% | 12.2 |
| **bolt** | 36.4% | 5.9% | 4.6% | 3.5% | 0.4% | 13.9% | 10.3% | 4.0% | 0.5% | 0.4% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 20.1% | 34.8 |

*Hand-written reading.* **Bolt spends five times as much of its match hunting as
Rusty does** — 5.9% of decisions against 1.2% — even though the branch is
identical for both and neither character has a dial that touches it. The
difference is everything *above* the branch. `hunt` sits below `defend`, and
Rusty's `defend` share is the only leaf that grows when the endgame is added
(11.8% → 15.3%): its home alarm is ringing, because Bolt is on its doorstep. So
the collapse is spent by one character answering an alarm and by the other
causing it, and the branch meant to give the tier an endgame gives it to
whichever character was already winning the pre-collapse exchange.

Note what the autopsy in §2.1 rules out: it is **not** that Bolt is under-built.
Both characters finish with about three turrets and a shield standing. Bolt
stands at the build wheel on 0.3% of its decisions and still gets its ring up,
because a purchase costs one tick and it only has to be docked for a few of them.
**Recklessness does not merely survive a contest. It owns one.**

So the branch is filed as evidence and reverted. `6ac4b692` takes it back out of
`src/bots` and keeps every artifact it produced; it can be re-run at any time with
`run.sh <label> easy <seeds> 8 --ref 7ca2d7f1`. I would like the Director to have
it — the gap is real and a match whose last minute and a half has no opponent in
it is worth fixing — but it must not ship *against this target*, and §11 says why
I think the target is the thing that should move first.

A control worth one line: `d-noflee-only` switches the retreat off during the
collapse and comes back bit-identical to the shipped run, because
`behaviors.ts` `wantsRetreat` already returns false when the field has closed.
The endgame result is the endgame's, not a retreat interaction.

### 6.2 · Everything else that was tried on Bolt

| lever | what it would cost the character | measured | verdict |

Every lever on this table either fails to reach the ceiling, hands the lead to
Rusty, makes Bolt worse at being Bolt for no gain, or makes the number worse.
There is no version of "turn Bolt's dial" that survives its own measurement.

## 8 · The decision

**No weight moves, no tier constant moves, no tree changes. `git diff
origin/main -- src/bots` is empty on this branch.**

The brief handed this lane a dial and asked, before turning it, which of three
things was holding the number up. The answer is the third — low caution is simply
strong at this skill — and the reason it is strong is not the one the question
implies. It is not that a reckless bot out-fights a timid one; nothing in this
pool fights for a reactor at all. It is that **the timid bot runs home, and
running home is what puts a reactor in front of the only gun on the field**. Bolt
is over by 17.1 points because it is the character that never went home.

Four things follow, and each of them is a measurement rather than an opinion:

1. **The dial the brief named is real and it is `caution`** — and `caution` 0.5
   *is* Bolt. §6.2 prices every other dial on the character and each one either
   misses the ceiling, costs the character something a player would notice, or
   makes the number worse.
2. **Turning it all the way does not demonstrate the target met.** At Rusty's
   `caution` the pool turns over and its top is Rusty at 53.7% —
   UNRESOLVED rather than INSIDE (§5.2), and settling which would take
   **6668 decided matches** — 17.2× this arm, and at its 81.1% draw rate that is **35287 matches to run**. A two-horse contest cannot be brought under a 55% ceiling by weakening
   one horse; it can only be brought inside 45–55%, which is a different and much
   harder request than the one GDD §3.8 wrote down.
3. **Raising the pool does not work either**, and that is measured, not assumed:
   arming Rusty triples the evidence and leaves Bolt at 69.4% (§3.1).
4. **The one change that is right on its own merits makes the target worse.** The
   Easy tier genuinely should contest the collapse; giving it that takes Bolt to
   95.2% (§6.1).

So the honest deliverable is the mechanism and the price list, and a request that
the target be re-specified before anybody is sent at it again — which is also
a0-126's own recommendation, arrived at from the other side.

## 9 · What a player would notice

Nothing, on this branch — and that is the point of §8, not an evasion of it. But
the brief asks what a player would notice about Bolt, and the measurements above
answer it better than the win rate does:

- **Bolt is not winning fights.** In the pool where it wins 72.1% of the
  decided matches it dies **32.5 times per seat per match** against
  Rusty's 11.8, and spends nearly a fifth of its decisions waiting on
  a respawn clock. What it wins is a comparison of reactor HP at the moment the
  field closes, and it wins that by about a hit point and a half.
- **Bolt is playing a different game from its tier.** Its most common decision by
  a wide margin is `potshot` (41.7%); it mines on 6.1% of decisions and stands at
  the build wheel on 0.3%. The tier's contract is "mines slowly, over-defends,
  attacks rarely". Bolt does none of the three. That is deliberate — GDD §2.9's
  "reckless rusher", and "Bolt barely turns around" is the design's own sentence
  — and it is worth a Director's eye anyway, because the tier's
  three-clause promise describes exactly one of its two characters.
- **A player in an all-Easy lobby watches the last ninety seconds happen without
  opponents.** Once the field closes, Rusty and Bolt keep mining rock that no
  longer spawns and defending a home that entropy is eating regardless. Medium
  and Hard bots come for you; Easy bots do not. That is the one thing in this
  report a player could actually see, and it is `7ca2d7f1`, sitting reverted on
  this branch with a price tag on it.

Nothing that has been *rejected* here would have made Bolt read better in a
cockpit. A Bolt with `caution` 1.3 breaks off at two-thirds hull; a Bolt with
`homebody` 0.8 answers its own alarm instead of flying at you; a Bolt with
`aimScale` 1.0 stops spraying. Each of those is a different character with Bolt's
name on it, and the last one would have made the number *worse* while doing it.

## 11 · Reading

**Bolt is over, the finding is durable, and it is not a balance problem — it is a
measurement pointed at the wrong quantity.**

- **Bolt, Easy pool — NO.** 72.1% of 1675 decided matches,
  exact 95% confidence interval 69.8% – 74.2%. a0-126 was right, and depth confirms
  it rather than dissolving it the way it dissolved Warden's.
- **Warden, cast contest — YES.** 51.7% (50.0% – 53.3%), unchanged and
  provably unchangeable by this branch (§7.2).
- **excavator, ship-class contest — YES.** 47.1%
  (44.0% – 50.2%), same.

**What I am asking the Director for, in one sentence:** re-specify the Easy-pool
target before another lane is sent at it, because a 55% ceiling applied to a
two-contestant pool whose winner is decided by a hit point and a half of
collateral damage is not measuring balance, and every honest attempt to satisfy
it either deletes the contest, moves the crown, or costs a character its
character.

Three concrete options, in the order I would take them:

1. **Make the pool a contest first, then measure it.** Ship `7ca2d7f1` — the
   Easy tier's missing collapse branch — *and* accept that the pool will read far
   over 55% until the characters are re-balanced against a game they are actually
   playing. Balancing a pool that draws four times in five is balancing noise;
   this is the option that fixes a real gameplay absence and buys a measurable
   pool as a side effect.
2. **Re-specify the ceiling by pool size.** A 55% ceiling means "no dominance"
   against a four-way fair share of 25%. Against a two-way fair share of 50% the
   equivalent statement is not 55% — it is something like "the top of a pool of
   *n* stays under `1/n + 0.30`", which gives the Easy pool 80% and the class
   contest its familiar 55%. That is one line in `harness/balance.ts` and it is
   QA's to write, not mine.
3. **Drop the equal-skill pools from the ceiling target entirely** and keep them
   as the diagnostic they are good at — the tier ladder and the draw rate. The
   contests a player actually meets are the cast contest and the ship-class
   contest, and both are comfortably inside the band.

And one thing that is not a request: **a0-126's UNRESOLVED verdict earned its
keep here.** Four of this report's fourteen arms came back
UNRESOLVED, and the `caution` 1.3 arm is the one that shows what that is worth. A
two-state convention would have read it as *"Bolt 42.9% — target met"* and
shipped the nerf; the three-state one reads it as a pool whose top is the other
character and whose interval still contains the ceiling, on evidence that cannot
settle it. Being allowed to file "the run does not know" is what let this brief
spend its matches on the mechanism instead of on a change it would not have been
able to defend the week after.
