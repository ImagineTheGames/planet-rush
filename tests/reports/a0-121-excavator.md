## 2 · What GDD §2.11 says the excavator pays

Quoted, in full, because this brief was written on the belief that the simulation
ignored it:

> | Class (hull) | Role · Speed / Accel / Turn / Hull / Power / Cargo |
> |---|---|
> | Excavator (Pincer) | Mining engine, close bruiser · **90% / 100% / 80%** / 55 / 13 / 2 |
>
> […] Baselines are opening hypotheses (**TUNABLE**), relative to the Vanguard
>
> The intended rock-paper-scissors around the triangle: the Interceptor catches
> miners in the open but melts against turrets; **the Excavator out-earns everyone
> but can't run** […] QA gains a third measurable target: **no class exceeds a 55%
> win rate** in mirror-vs-field harness runs.

So the stated penalty is exactly two numbers — **top speed 90%** and **turn rate
80%**, both relative to the Vanguard — plus one sentence of intent, *"out-earns
everyone but can't run."*

**And §2.11 is vague about the size of it, in the one way that matters.** It states
the penalty as *ratios* and never states the absolutes those ratios multiply.
`BASE_SPEED` (260 u/s), `BASE_ACCEL` (900 u/s²) and `BASE_TURN_RATE`
(6.5 rad/s) are not §2.11 values — they are derived-physics
constants that §2.8 does not carry either, flagged TUNABLE and handed over with no
target attached to them. A ratio is only a penalty if the quantity it scales is
scarce. §2.11 fixes the ratio and leaves the scarcity to a constant it never
mentions, and **that gap is this whole brief.** It is reported here as the finding
the brief asked for: the design cannot be implemented precisely as written,
because as written it does not say how much 80% is supposed to hurt.

### 3.1 · The design column beside the simulation column — the tree a0-121 was handed

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `interceptor` | 130% / 120% / 140% | 338.0 u/s | 1080 u/s² | 9.10 rad/s | **0.345 s** | 0.621 s |
| `vanguard` | 100% / 100% / 100% | 260.0 u/s | 900 u/s² | 6.50 rad/s | **0.483 s** | 0.505 s |
| `excavator` | 90% / 100% / 80% | 234.0 u/s | 900 u/s² | 5.20 rad/s | **0.604 s** | 0.404 s |
| `hauler` | 85% / 80% / 85% | 221.0 u/s | 720 u/s² | 5.52 rad/s | **0.569 s** | 0.588 s |

Two things to read off it before anything else.

**The 80% is 0.12 seconds.** The Excavator comes about in 0.60 s
against the Vanguard's 0.48 s. That is the entire penalty §2.11 states on
this column, and it is a third of one `fireInterval` (0.35 s).

**The hull with the mobility penalty is the most responsive hull on the board.**
§2.11 gives it accel 100% and top speed 90%; the simulation applies both
faithfully, and the composition is that it reaches 90% of its own top speed in
0.404 s — sooner than the Vanguard (0.505 s) and sooner than the Interceptor
(0.621 s), which §2.11 gives 120% acceleration. Nothing is mis-implemented here:
a lower cap under the same thrust is simply reached earlier. It is reported
because "can't run" reads as a claim about getting away, and the stat a player
feels when getting away from a standing start is this one.

## 8 · Reading

*Hand-written. Every number it quotes is in a table above.*

### 8.1 · The brief's premise, corrected

a0-117 and a0-120 both handed on the same sentence — *"a speed-and-turn penalty
the simulation charges nothing for"* — and this brief repeats it as *"the sim does
not make it so."* It is not what the tree does. §3.2 reads all twenty-four cells
of §2.11 back out of `SHIP_STATS` and every one of them matches, the Excavator's
90% and 80% included; `shipTopSpeed` and `shipTurnRate` apply them on every tick.

The true statement is narrower and more useful: **§2.11 states the penalty as a
ratio and never states the absolute the ratio multiplies.** `BASE_SPEED`,
`BASE_ACCEL` and `BASE_TURN_RATE` are derived-physics constants that neither
§2.11 nor the §2.8 table carries — flagged TUNABLE with no target attached. A
ratio is a penalty only if the quantity under it is scarce, and at 6.5 rad/s
turning is not scarce for anybody. That is the finding the brief asked for when it
said a design that cannot be implemented precisely is a finding: **§2.11 is not
vague about the penalty's shape, it is vague about its size**, and the size is
where all of the missing 22 points were.

### 8.2 · Whether turn rate should vary by hull at all

Yes, and it already does — but only in the range where it binds. §6 and the
golden-bite proof measure the same threshold from two directions. `turnToward`
clamps to the target angle the moment the gap falls under one tick's step, so
above roughly 2 rad/s a hull is on target within two or three ticks and its rate
stops being observable at all: perturbing the **Interceptor's** `turnMul` from 1.4
to 1.41 does not move a single one of three 180-second state hashes, while the
same proportional nudge on the Excavator's retuned 0.25 fails all three
(`evidence/a0-121-excavator-penalty/bite.txt`, blocks 2 and 4a/4b). §2.11's
140/100/80/85 spread was, at `BASE_TURN_RATE` 6.5, four numbers the simulation
could not tell apart.

So the answer is that the column should vary by hull, that the design already says
so, and that what the design left out is how much a "point" of it is worth. This
branch answers that for one cell, by measurement, and leaves the base alone.

### 8.3 · What a turn rate can and cannot implement

*"Out-earns everyone but can't run"* cannot be implemented by this column, at any
value, and that is a structural fact rather than a tuning one. `integrate` in
`src/sim/step.ts` adds `intent.thrust × accel` in **world** space and never reads
`ship.angle`: thrust and aim are independent verbs (GDD §2.4's six-verb table), so
movement is holonomic and a hull's facing does not gate, scale or delay where it
goes. A ship reverses its travel exactly as fast whichever way its nose is
pointing — and the Excavator, having less momentum to shed, reverses *sooner* than
the Interceptor that turns 1.75× faster.

The turn column therefore implements **slow to bring the gun to bear**, and only
that. It is real — bots aim manually (`src/bots/steering.ts` emits `fire{auto:
false}`, a0-117's finding) so a bot's shot goes down its barrel — and at 0.25 it
is finally large enough to be paid. *"Can't run"* is a **top-speed** claim, and
a0-120 measured that `BASE_SPEED` 234 vs 260 only starts to bite in an arena of
about 3150 units, which is larger than the arena a player can see across.

Both facts are now pinned as tests rather than left as prose: `src/sim/step.test.ts`,
*"facing does not gate travel — the turn column is a nose stat"*.

### 8.4 · What moves for the other three hulls

Nothing in the class table (§7.4): the Interceptor, Vanguard and Hauler keep
`BASE_TURN_RATE` × their own §2.11 multiplier, and every other cell of §2.11 is
untouched for every hull. What moves is their **share of the wins the Excavator
stopped taking**, which is the point of the exercise:

- Vanguard 10.9% → 27.5%, Hauler 9.0% → 20.4% (§7.1). The board goes from
  77.3 / 10.9 / 9.0 / 2.7 to 48.6 / 27.5 / 20.4 / 3.5 against a 25% fair share.
- **The Interceptor is the honest residue: 2.7% → 3.5%**, which is inside the
  noise. It is 280 on `dps × hp` against the Excavator's 715 (§3.3) and it loses
  every duel in the game; nothing in this brief's scope reaches that, and no
  reading here should be taken to claim it did.
- On the cast board the Interceptor does move, 2.7% → 9.0% (§7.2), because a cast
  board is not a mirror: the hull it is now catching is one that can no longer
  turn to answer.

### 8.5 · Why `power` was not the cell that moved

§4 measures `power` 13 → 11 at 42.2% and 13 → 10 at 18.8%, monotone and
predictable, and §3.3 shows the surplus does live there — one stat that "one
weapon, one stat" (§2.5) counts twice, into mining rate *and* weapon damage, on
the hull that also has the second-best armour. It is the better dial on every
technical measure.

It was not taken because it contradicts the design's own sentence. §2.11 makes
this hull the *"Mining engine"* that *"out-earns everyone"*; `power` is the stat
that says so. Cutting it fixes the win rate by deleting the identity, whereas the
brief's instruction — and §2.11's prose — is that this hull is supposed to
out-earn and pay for it in mobility. **The penalty column was the one the design
nominated, so the penalty column is the one that moved.** If the Director prefers
the other, §4 is the priced version of that ruling and needs no further
measurement.

### 8.6 · What needs a ruling, and what does not

**Does not:** the value itself. GDD §2.11 calls the class table *"opening
hypotheses (**TUNABLE**), relative to the Vanguard"* and, in the same section,
states the acceptance test those hypotheses must meet — *"no class exceeds a 55%
win rate in mirror-vs-field harness runs."* An opening hypothesis that fails its
own stated test by 22 points, for free, is exactly what a TUNABLE is for, and
`src/sim/constants.ts` is the table this lane owns.

**Does:** the printed figure. §2.11's table says 80% and the simulation now says
25%. The game is self-consistent either way — the lobby's ship-select tiles read
the class table, so a tile can never show a pip beside a number that means
something else (§2.11's own guarantee) — but the **document is behind**, and only
the Director can fold it back. The divergence is asserted rather than hidden:
`src/sim/upgrades.test.ts` still transcribes §2.11 by hand, names this one cell as
`PENDING_AMENDMENT`, compares every *other* cell against the document, and fails
the day the GDD catches up so the exception can be deleted.

**Also worth a ruling, and not this brief's to make:** §3.3's total order. Three
lanes have now been sent at the Excavator's win rate and none of them was allowed
to look at the fact that the *"scout, miner-hunter"* cannot win a fight with
anything. That is a §2.11 triangle that is not a triangle, and it is a hull tile's
worth of design work rather than a constant.
