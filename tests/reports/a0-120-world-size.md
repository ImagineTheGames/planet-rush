*Hand-written. Every number it quotes is in a table below.*

### The two curves, and the 350 units between them

There is one dial and it is pulling in two directions, so the whole finding is two
monotone curves and where they cross the lines that matter:

| `WORLD_SIZE` | `excavator`, ship-class contest | blind berths (of 6300) | both? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2400 (shipped) | 77.3% | 0 | ore field ✓, hull ✗ |
| 2807 | **69.1%** | **0** | ore field ✓, hull ✗ |
| 2808 | — | 35 | — |
| 3199 | **54.7%** | 2100 | hull ✓, ore field ✗ |
| 3400 (a0-117's candidate) | 51.8% *(a0-117)* | 4060 | hull ✓, ore field ✗✗ |

The hull needs roughly 3150 and the ore field allows at most 2807. **The gap is
about 350 world units and it is not a measurement uncertainty** — 2807 is exact
(2808 blinds a berth) and the hull's crossing is bracketed by two 254-match
readings, 69.1% at 2807 and 54.7% at 3199. Nothing in between can satisfy both.

### 2807 is a real number and it is not the number a0-117 had

a0-117's ceiling deserves its correction stated plainly rather than buried, because
its reasoning was right and only its sample was narrow. It read the limit off
`src/sim/radar-fog.test.ts` — a good instrument for the question, but it builds
**one board**: two players, `octagon`, seed 7. Measured across all three maps whose
bounds are `WORLD_SIZE`, at 2–8 players and 60 seeds (2100 berths per map):

- **`crescents` 2807** — the binding one, and a shipped, pickable board. It is one
  of the two two-sided team layouts (`TEAM_MAP_IDS`), it appears as a card in the
  m8-02 picker like every other map (`src/ui/map-picker.ts` — `cards: MAPS.map(…)`,
  no mode gate), and its ring sits at 916 from centre where `octagon`'s sits at 864.
- **`octagon` 3199** — a0-117's number, reproduced to the unit: `radar-fog` passes
  at 3100 and fails at 3200 because 3199 is where the last `octagon` berth is still
  inside 520 units of a rock it owns.
- **`compass` 3735** — the roomiest, because its inner-square layout puts four of
  its eight homes 648 from centre and the home field is stamped against the
  *smallest* station radius (`sim/waves.ts`, the `singleRing` branch's `else`).
- `oval`, `diamond` and `line` sit on the hardcoded `WIDE` 3200×2000 and this dial
  does not reach them at all.

**A ceiling nobody measured above is a guess**, so `src/bots/berth-ore-visibility.test.ts`
pins each of the three at the unit: it asserts every berth still sees its field at
the number and that at least one does not one unit past it. That file is the
tripwire the next tuning pass will hit before it gets as far as this report.

### The bot does not go blind first, and that was worth checking

Fog honesty (GDD §2.9) is usually read as a ceiling on what a bot may know. This is
the other edge of it: a bot picks a mining site out of `BotView.perception.visualRange`
(720), a player's minimap draws ore inside `SHIP_SENSOR_RANGE` (520), and if the
arena ever grew past the point where those two orderings swap, bots would be
choosing rocks their human opponents cannot see. They do not swap anywhere near
here — the bot-side ceilings are 3511 / 4299 / 4731 against the fog's 2807 / 3199 /
3735, so the human's map goes dark first on every map, by 700 units at the worst.
Pinned as a case rather than a comment, because it is an invariant and not a
coincidence.

### The brief's order was right, and the characters do come with the hull

The brief said measure the hull first and re-measure the characters afterwards,
because Warden 73.1% and Bolt 83.3% were read on a cast that can pick the
`excavator`. That is exactly what happens, and no character tree was touched to
make it happen (§3.3):

- **Warden 73.1% → 59.8% at 2807 → 32.4% at 3199.** At 3199 it is comfortably
  inside; at 2807, the largest arena that keeps the ore field, it is **still over at
  59.8%**. So even the character target does not come inside on a legal value.
- **Foreman — the other `excavator` — becomes the top of the cast at 3199 (38.3%)**,
  and the cast spreads seven ways instead of one character taking three matches in
  four.
- **The `excavator` silhouette still takes 70.7% of the *cast* contest at 3199**
  against 54.7% of the *ship-class* contest, and a0-117's explanation of that gap
  stands: the cast seats two excavators of eight, so the hull's share of a cast
  board is structurally higher than its share of an equal-hull board. One dial does
  not close both, and no arena size will.

### And the equal-skill pools say the dial does not only fix things

This is the part of §3.5 that has to be read before anyone reaches for the
`octagon`-only reading in §5. Growing the arena does not merely bring the
`excavator` down; it re-sorts the equal-skill pools, and at 3199 it pushes **two
contestants that were comfortably inside the band out of it**:

- **Vulture goes 28.1% → 38.5% → 61.1%** in the Hard pool. It is the "catches
  miners in the open" archetype and a bigger board is exactly what it wants, so
  the direction is unsurprising — the magnitude is that at 3199 it takes 58 of 95
  decided matches and Sable collapses to 9.5%. A three-character pool that read
  37.5 / 34.4 / 28.1 on the shipped tree reads 61.1 / 29.5 / 9.5.
- **Patch was already over and stays over** — 62.0% shipped, 60.4% at 2807, 64.3%
  at 3199, in a two-character pool whose fair share is 50%. a0-112 called its 59.4%
  "1.5 SE and not yet evidence of anything"; three independent draws on the same
  side of the line is more than that now, and no value of this dial helps it.
- **Bolt cannot be measured on any candidate, and barely on the shipped tree.**
  The easy pool decides 12 of 64 matches at 2400, **7 of 64 at 2807 and 1 of 64 at
  3199**. Bolt's 83.3% is 10 of those 12; at 2807 it is 4 of 7 (57.1%, ±1 SE = 18.7
  points) and at 3199 it is 1 of 1. Those are not win rates and must not be quoted
  as any. a0-117 said it and a0-120 confirms it on two more arenas: **two Easy bots
  on one hull do not finish each other**, and the arena dial makes that worse
  monotonically. It is a Bots brief about the Easy tree — not a constant, and the
  one of the three targets a constant was never going to reach.

So the trade at 3199 is not "one contestant comes inside the band". It is **one
comes in, one goes out, one stays out, and one stops being measurable** — bought
with 2100 blind berths. That is worth knowing before the `octagon`-only reading is
taken as the cheap way out.

### Why the golden was not re-baselined — and what proving it bites turned up

The brief's instruction was to re-baseline `src/bots/ffa-parity.test.ts` from the
merged tree and to **show the test still fails if you perturb an input it is meant
to catch**, because "a golden you re-baked without proving it still bites is a
deleted test."

**No hash was re-baked, because `WORLD_SIZE` did not move.** Re-baselining a
determinism golden for a constant change that is not happening would be exactly the
move the brief warns about, with no change to point at.

The proof was run anyway, first, on the merged tree — six perturbations and two
controls, in `evidence/a0-120-parity-bites/bite.txt`. **The three hashes bite.**
`WORLD_SIZE` 2400 → 2401, one world unit, fails all three (a0-117 §4 reproduced).
So does 2400 → 2807. So does one Easy personality weight moved far enough for a
180-second match to act on it — `caution` 1.3 → 0.2 gives `45432f00 / d557756e /
340076b0` against the goldens' `d839695f / df4873c3 / b3055735`. (1.3 → 1.31 does
*not*, and that is resolution rather than a hole: +0.8% on one weight flips no
comparison inside three minutes of discrete decisions. It is recorded so the next
pass does not mistake a null perturbation for a dead test.)

**The fourth case did not bite, and it had not for two weeks.** The last assertion
in the file — the guard on the guard, the only thing standing between three hash
literals and a "teams" world that is secretly an FFA world — read:

```
expect(teamed).not.toBe('6d78b590');
```

`6d78b590` is the **pre-a0-05 FFA hash**, from the first table in that file's own
module note. It was retired by the first of six re-baselines on 2026-08-07 and no
build since has produced it. Every one of those six re-baselines moved the three
goldens above it and left this literal where it was, so the assertion had been
comparing a live hash against a number no world could return. Staged against Trap 1
in its full form — every seat's side dropped, so world table, bot radios and ally
lists are all FFA and `teamed` comes back as `d839695f`, the FFA golden itself — it
**passed** (§4 of the evidence). The fixed version reads the FFA side live and
compares against that, fails on the identical perturbation (§5), and re-baselines
itself, so it cannot rot behind the goldens a seventh time. No hash moved to fix it.

That is the case for doing the proof before the re-bake rather than after: this
brief did not re-baseline anything, and the exercise still found a deleted test.

## 5 · What a structural fix would need

*Hand-written.*

**1 · Scale the sensor with the arena — the cheapest, and it is not this lane's.**
Every distance the arena *contains* is a fraction of `WORLD_SIZE`; `SHIP_SENSOR_RANGE`
(520) and `STATION_SENSOR_RANGE` (300) are absolute. That asymmetry is the whole
ceiling, and closing it removes the constraint rather than trading against it.
The exact requirement, measured rather than estimated:

| `WORLD_SIZE` | `SHIP_SENSOR_RANGE` required | as a multiple of 520 | proportional rule `520 × W/2400` | vs satellite 900 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2807 | **520** | ×0.999 | 608 ✓ covers it | 1.73× (is 1.73× today) |
| 3199 | **632** | ×1.214 | 693 ✓ covers it | 1.42× (is 1.73× today) |
| 3400 | **689** | ×1.323 | 737 ✓ covers it | 1.31× (is 1.73× today) |

a0-117 estimated "roughly ×1.42: 740 and 425" from the arena ratio; the measured
requirement at 3400 is **689**, so the proportional rule `520 × W / 2400` covers it
with margin at every size. **It is not free, and the cost is not the constant.** A
radar satellite's reach (900) is worth ore because a ship's own bubble is small —
GDD §2.6, and `SHIP_SENSOR_RANGE`'s own note, "sits comfortably under a satellite's
`sensorRange`". At 689 that margin falls from **1.73× to 1.31×**, which devalues a
building the economy is balanced around. Both radii are `content/codex/*.json` facts
enforced by `tests/codex/codex-constants.test.ts` and a GDD §2.8 table row, so this
is a Gameplay/content amendment, not a QA dial and not a Bots one.

**2 · Move the home field out toward the berth — cheaper still, and it re-opens a
closed field report.** The field sits at 0.44–0.47 of the ring radius while the
berth sits at ring − 96, so most of the gap is deliberate. Closing it means pushing
`homeOuterFraction` to roughly **0.55** at 3400 — back inside the 0.52–0.64 band
that field report P1 explicitly retired ("at 0.64 the nearest rock sat only ~215 u
from the ship's spawn point, so a ship leaving spawn bumped rock"). It also runs
into `SPAWN_CLEAR_POCKET`, which clamps the band's outer edge on purpose. Priced
here so the next pass does not have to re-derive it; not recommended.

**3 · Make the `excavator`'s stated penalty real — the fix the design already
implies.** This is a0-117's own alternative and a0-120's measurements do not
disturb it: GDD §2.11 gives one hull the best power stat *and* the second-best
speed, against a speed-and-turn penalty the simulation charges nothing for, because
at `BASE_TURN_RATE` 6.5 a hull flips 180° in 0.48 s. "Out-earns everyone but can't
run" has no mechanism behind it. A firing arc, a turn-gated weapon or a re-costed
class row would make the penalty real without asking the arena to do it — and,
unlike the arena, it would close the *cast*-board gap (§3.2) as well as the
equal-hull one, which no value of `WORLD_SIZE` does. It is a gameplay change and
needs its own brief.

**And what does NOT need a brief: `WORLD_SIZE` alone.** After a0-117 priced every
lever and a0-120 measured the one that works against the constraint that stops it,
the honest summary is that this constant cannot deliver the §2.11 target at all —
